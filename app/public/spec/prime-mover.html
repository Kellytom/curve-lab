1 · What the page is: one walk

The walk is the page. Everything else is a picture of it or derived from it.

apex      (24, 7)              the first hypotenuse, on radius 25
rotation  (527, 336, 625)      the double angle, 32.5204°: 527 + 336i = (24 + 7i)²
stations  twelve, on the 25 circle, equally spaced
  • The walk is the orbit of an apex under the rotation, one model for every triple.
  • Every station is a reduced ratio of whole numbers: x a multiple of 24, y a multiple of 7, the hypotenuse a power of 5.
  • The rotation is the square of the triangle's own turn, because two triangles share each apex.
  • Twelve stations reach 357.7°, 2.3° short of a full turn; the shortfall is stated rather than hidden.
  • The station count follows the triple, and the page prints it (23 stations for the default half walk).

8 · The geometry, stated

Everything in Figs 1–4 follows from these facts; they are written here so the figures can be checked against them rather than against an eye.

1. Two circles and a triangle. C25 radius 25 and C24 radius 24 about O, with |OF| = 24, |FA| = 7, |OA| = 25, right angle at F. cos φ = 24/25, so both coordinates are ratios.

2. Reflection keeps the circle. Every mirror line passes through O, so reflection preserves the distance to O: every apex stays on C25 and every foot on C24.

3. Two reflections are one rotation. Consecutive triangles advance the apex by 2φ = 32.5204°, and (cos 2φ, sin 2φ) = (527/625, 336/625), hence 527² + 336² = 625².

4. Parity is conserved. x′ = (527x − 336y)/625 stays even, y′ = (336x + 527y)/625 stays odd, because 625ᵏ is odd — the numerators end in 4 or 6 and in 3 or 7.

5. The integers behind the fractions. x is always a multiple of 24 = 2³·3, y a multiple of 7, and x² + y² = (25 · 625ᵏ)²: a p-triple at every level.

6. It cannot close evenly. 360 / 32.5204 = 11.07, so twelve apices stop 2.3° short; the spacing between consecutive apices is exactly equal, and that is what the check measures.

  • The first apex is (24, 7); every apex is on C25 within 0.1 unit.
  • The gap between consecutive apices is 32.52° within 0.01°.
  • The figures are square, because a circle drawn with two scales is an ellipse.
  • Fig. 2 carries visible axes named x and y, a zero line and whole-number grid.

9 · Priority: the circle the walk cannot see

The standing priority of the page. A curve is rebuilt on the exact lattice by taking its circle out and reading what is left at the walk's stations.

  1. Write F(x, y) = 0 and split it into the circular part C — every term c·(x² + y²) — and the remainder R, so F = C + R.
  2. On the walk x² + y² = 1 at every station, so C is the same constant everywhere and the walk is blind to it.
  3. The character, as the walk reads it, is R alone.
  • The quadratic split is implemented and checked exactly: x² + 2xy + y² gives blind part 1 and remainder 2xy.
  • The general split reduces any polynomial by x² + y² = 1 to a(y) + x·b(y); (x² + y²)² gives blind part 1 and no remainder terms.
  • A PH speed is recognised as a circle walk: x' = u² − v², y' = 2uv, radius u² + v²; the tangent has norm 1 exactly on all three stored lace curves.
  • The sharp edge is stated: 2xy = 1 with x² + y² = 1 needs x = 1/√2, irrational, so no station lands on it and the figure prints a tolerance instead.
  • Every identity is asserted in Fraction, never in a float.

12 · Taking the circle out of a parametric curve

The operator's words, quoted so they are not paraphrased away:

"what does the spec say about taking the x^2+y^2 out of the parametric polynomials in the database we choose to display? what does it say about plotting that curve, not the old curve?"

"take a nice curve from the database, and plug it in to this generator … take a circle out of it … so that when you plugged in the new i values based on the circle, the curve would be reformed."

The rule. For x(t), y(t): form P(t) = x(t)² + y(t)²; the circular part is its mean C = ∫₀¹ P dt (the constant term is useless — every lace curve starts at the origin); the remainder is R = P − C; the reformed point is z·R/P.

What that does, said plainly. R/P = 1 − C/P depends only on distance, so the point keeps its direction and only its radius changes. A radial term cannot bend a parametric curve. A circle is the case P ≡ C, where R ≡ 0 and the curve collapses to the origin.

  • The operation is implemented exactly in scripts/circle_plots.py.
  • The curve is plotted with its stations set by the circle parameter t = Y/(1+X), kept to 0 ≤ t ≤ 1, with the stations as exact rational points.
  • The remainder is plotted in the bounded form (P − C)/(P + C): zero where the circle carries the curve, positive outside the mean circle, negative inside.
  • The mean always pinches, and that changes the outline. C is the mean of P, so it lies between the curve's nearest and farthest point; somewhere P = C and the reformed point is exactly the origin. Where P < C the factor is negative, so the point comes out on the opposite ray. The directions are unchanged, but the figure is a new one, and crossings can appear across the origin. Superseded 2026-11-20: the earlier line here said "the shape cannot change, so no figure claims a new outline"; that is true of the re-stationing (t = Y/(1+X)) and false of the mean-C reform. Fig. 10 shows the difference, and both are stated on the figure.

15 · How this page thinks: algorithmically, geometrically

The standing method, in the operator's words: "you need to think algorithmically and geometrically." Every figure on this page is produced by a construction that can be written down, not by hand-placed shapes.

The first algorithmic idea — reform a database curve with the circle's values. Take a mature curve with character from the database, take x² + y² out of it, then plug in the "new i values based on the circle". The circle is the object the walk can express exactly, so the reform is: station the curve at t = Y/(1+X) from the walk, which makes every station a rational point of the lattice.

  • The rule is stated (§12) and implemented (scripts/circle_plots.py).
  • The figure prints the original parametric rows and the reformed formula beside the drawing, so the reader can judge whether the reform is simpler.
  • Two operations, kept apart: the re-stationing (t = Y/(1+X)) leaves the outline alone and is what c-0272 stores; the mean-C reform (z·(P − C)/P) moves every point along its ray and changes the outline, and §12 states where it pinches.
  • Fig. 10 · the table, and the circle taken out. The stations are the driver's own table — k, a, b, denominator, both directions, the same rule as driverRows() — written by scripts/pm_reform_probe.py to workbench/pm-reform.json. The lens map x = X(1 − Y²), y = Y is drawn, then the same stations carried through z·(P − C)/P. The stations are exact ratios; the line between them is sampled and says so; C is printed exactly and as ≈.
  • The guard is app/scripts/check-prime-mover-page.mjs: Fig. 10 draws two panels, each names x and y, and each carries a legend at 15 px or larger.
  • A stored curve is reformed and saved back to the database as its own record: c-0272 lace-2-eight-3-circle-parameter, a path of 61 exact stations taken at t = k/60 on c-0028, each with its own rational circle point; the note states the parameter and C, its row is in workbench/circle-split.json, and tests/test_circle_split.py (test_way_4_the_reformed_curve_is_saved_as_its_own_record) fails without it.

The second algorithmic idea — make rosettes with linkages. A coupler curve is asymmetric; the crank run backwards gives its mirror; the pair is a rosette made by a mechanism rather than sampled from a spirograph.

  • The exactness condition is a rule, not a hope: a joint is rational only when the closure quadratic has a rational square discriminant (§10).
  • The search found the exact family: 60 parallelogram link sets close at every one of 20 rational crank directions.
  • That family is drawn on the page; Fig. 8 remains the sampled crank-rocker.

Two habits that follow, and are checked:

  • A diagram never appears without its formula: every Fig. 9 panel prints its rows, its remainder and its reform under the drawing.
  • An idea becomes a check or it is a wish: each method above names the script and the test or page check that fails when it stops being true.
