What the page is for

Stage I supplies a prime mover: a point on the circle at every station, exact, with coordinates X and Y. Stage II turns those two numbers into a curve.

The curve is a polynomial of X and Y with whole-number coefficients:

px = p(X, Y)
py = q(X, Y)

Because X and Y are ratios of whole numbers and the coefficients are whole numbers, every curve point is a ratio of whole numbers. Nothing is sampled on the way in; the only sampling is the straight line drawn between one exact station and the next, and the page says so.

The spirograph (Fig. 2)

Two vanes from one generator, R = (24 + 7i)/25:

outer   a·Rᵏ          one turn a station
inner   b·R^(m·k)     m turns a station, m a whole number
curve   the sum of the two

The ratio m must be whole, or the pattern never repeats — two different p-triples have an irrational ratio and produce a smear, not a spirograph. Every station is exact, because R has length exactly one.

The figure draws the sum in ink, the outer vane in orange and the inner in blue, so the shape can be read back to the arms that made it. Ratio chooser: 3, 5, 7, 9.

Rules: three paths (sum, outer, inner); the title names the ratio and the station count; the ratio chooser changes both.

The presets

preset px py what it shows
the circle X Y the prime mover itself — the baseline
the S X Y − Y³ the fold that turns a circle into an S
the loop X − X³ Y a loop where the walk turns back on itself
the leaf X·Y Y two lobes, joined at the axis
the crest X X·Y a wave that leans with the height

Each is whole-number arithmetic on ratios, so each stays exact. The presets are named for what they look like, not for the polynomial degree.

What the page shows, top to bottom

  1. A title and a lead saying that this page feeds the prime mover into a polynomial.
  2. One chooser — the preset. The walk is fixed: the twelve hit points of walker(k), so there is no step chooser to send it somewhere else.
  3. Fig. 1, the curve — the preset's curve through the exact stations, with the circle drawn faintly behind it as the baseline. The figure carries its own title, names the preset and the step, and has x and y axes.
  4. A readout — clicking a station names its i, x and y in exact ratios.
  5. The station table — i, px, py for the first stations, every value a whole number or a ratio of two whole numbers.

Drawn as curves, not as zigzags

Every plotted sequence joins its exact stations with a cubic Bézier chain (Catmull-Rom through the points). The stations are exact; the line between them is approximate, and the figure says so. Straight segments between correct points are still a wrong picture.

The rules the page must keep

  1. Every curve point is an exact ratio. The table holds no decimals.
  2. The baseline circle is always drawn, faintly, so the deformation is visible — and it carries no lines of its own. No chords, no radii, no walk drawn on it. It is a reference, not a drawing: the only line in Fig. 1 is the mapped curve. (Same rule as the prime mover's ring: a line that carries no information is decoration, and decoration across a circle hides the one thing the circle is for.)
  3. Both choosers drive the drawing and the table — change either and the figure changes.
  4. The figure names the preset and the step inside it.
  5. No control does nothing.
  6. No complex step, no imaginary unit. Curves are px and py, never z.
  7. One minus sign: U+2212, never a hyphen.
  8. The drawn line between stations is a sampled line, and the page says so.
