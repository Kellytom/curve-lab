Prior art: curves driven by a point on a circle

Question: what does the literature call a parametric curve whose parameter is an angle on a circle, x(θ) = f(cos θ, sin θ), instead of a linear t? And has anyone forced such a curve onto an integer lattice?

Searched 2026-11-19 (web search only; no paywalled full texts read). Every "integer lattice" verdict below is "found nothing", not "proved absent".

The short answer

Name in the literature What it is On an integer lattice?

No prior art found for the whole combination: an integer-grid family of curves whose parameter steps by rational points of a circle. The mechanism is classical; the application to a grid family is the gap. Say "no prior art found in a 2026-11-19 web search", never "novel".

1. Circular harmonics

Definition. A 2-D shape or closed curve written as a sum of terms a_k cos kθ + b_k sin kθ, the planar twin of spherical harmonics; the curve is the sum of its harmonics, each harmonic one circular motion.

Citation. Circular Harmonics, valdes.cc — https://valdes.cc/articles/ch.html (the same objects appear in shape-matching literature as the frequency-domain form of a Fourier descriptor; see 2).

Integer lattice. No. Coefficients are real magnitudes; the harmonics are exact circles, so a "harmonic" on a lattice would need its own radius to be a lattice length. Nothing found.

Note the name collision: "circular harmonics" also means a pitch-class / tonal analysis in music theory. Do not cite that meaning for geometry.

2. Fourier descriptors and epicycles

Definition. A closed curve sampled as z(s) = x(s) + i y(s); its discrete Fourier coefficients are complex amplitudes, and the inverse transform draws the curve as one rotating circle (an epicycle) per coefficient — a chain of circles each carried by the last.

Citation. C. T. Zahn and R. Z. Roskies, "Fourier Descriptors for Plane Closed Curves", IEEE Trans. Computers C-21 (1972) — PDF: https://slac.stanford.edu/pubs/slacpubs/0750/slac-pub-0850.pdf Interactive epicycle view: https://www.dynamicmath.xyz/fourier-epicycles/

Integer lattice. No. This is the nearest thing to our construction in the literature: the epicycle chain is exactly "walk a circle by an angle". Every amplitude is a real number, and the standard use is shape matching, not exact integer geometry.

3. Rational parametrisation of hypotrochoids and epitrochoids

Definition. A trochoid is the path of a point fixed to a circle rolling on (great) or inside (hypo) a fixed circle: x = (R−r)cos θ + d cos((R−r)θ/r), y = (R−r)sin θ − d sin((R−r)θ/r).

Citation. Mathcurve, "Hypotrochoid" — https://mathcurve.com/courbes2d.gb/hypotrochoid/hypotrochoid.shtml Classical: R. C. Archibald, "The Cardioide and Some of Its Related Curves" (1900), still the standard historical survey (unverified detail). Epitrochoid family overview: https://www.walter-fendt.de/html5/men/epihypotrochoids_en.htm

Integer lattice. Not as trochoids. But: a trochoid with rational R/r is an algebraic curve, hence rationally parametrisable (genus 0 when it has a rational point, e.g. an epicycloid/hypocycloid cusp). A rational parametrisation is an integer-lattice statement — see 7. The rolling circle is the Ptolemaios epicycle; the "on a circle, odd closing" behaviour is the rational-ratio condition, same as Lissajous (5).

4. Polynomial / rational curves with a trigonometric parameter (trigonometric Bézier)

Definition. A Bézier-type basis built from sin, cos and 1 instead of (1−t)^k t^(n−k), so that a segment can be an exact arc of a circle; the uniform cubic "trigonometric Bézier" basis is {sin²u, cos²u, ...} with control points, plus two shape parameters.

Citation. X.-A. Han, Y. Ma, X.-L. Huang, "The cubic trigonometric Bézier curve with two shape parameters", Applied Mathematics Letters 22 (2009) — https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0893965908001262 Recent survey-adjacent item: "Quantum Trigonometric Bézier Curves", arXiv:2510.09336 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09336

Integer lattice. No. These are design curves with real control points; the trig basis exists so the circle comes out exactly, which is the opposite worry from keeping coordinates whole.

5. Lissajous curves, rational frequency ratios, Chebyshev form

Definition. x = A sin(a t + δ), y = B sin(b t); closed exactly when a/b is rational, and when a, b are integers the figure is an algebraic curve whose implicit equation is a polynomial in x, y via cos(nθ) = T_n(cos θ).

Citations. Wikipedia, "Lissajous curve" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lissajous_curve (closed-iff-rational, Chebyshev identity). Wolfram MathWorld, "Lissajous Curve" — https://mathworld.wolfram.com/LissajousCurve.html (periodic iff the frequency ratio is rational; implicitisation by Chebyshev polynomials). Erb et al. on Lissajous curves with integer frequencies as cubature nodes: Dolomites Research Notes on Approximation (2015) — https://arxiv.org/abs/… (the exact item was seen only through an aggregator page; unverified).

Integer lattice. Partly the closest match, but as sampling nodes, not as lattice points: the Chebyshev-Lissajous nodes used for cubature have exact algebraic (not integer) coordinates. No integer-lattice Lissajous found.

6. Chebyshev / trigonometric parametrisation

Definition. Using T_n(cos θ) = cos nθ to trade a trigonometric polynomial for a polynomial one; a curve given by two trigonometric polynomials of degree n becomes an algebraic curve of degree n, and conversely every curve with a trigonometric parametrisation is algebraic.

Citation. T. J. Rivlin, The Chebyshev Polynomials (Wiley, 1974); the identity and its use in Lissajous implicitisation as cited in 5. Chebyshev's own linkage work is the historical root: "Chebyshev linkage", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chebyshev_linkage

Integer lattice. Only the integer Chebyshev problem exists — minimising ‖p‖ over integer-coefficient polynomials on [−1,1] (Borwein & Erdélyi, Polynomials and Polynomial Inequalities, 1995). That is a different question (coefficients integer, curve not on a lattice). No Chebyshev-parametrised curve on a lattice found.

7. The bridge that does reach integers: stereographic / Pythagorean parametrisation

Definition. Every rational point of the unit circle is ((1−t²)/(1+t²), 2t/(1+t²)); with t = p/q this is ((q²−p²), 2pq, q²+p²)/|·|, i.e. a Pythagorean triple. This is the only standard way a circle-driven parameter becomes exact on a lattice.

Citations. "Pythagorean triple — rational points on the unit circle", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_triple "Rational curve" / conic parametrisation by a line of slope t, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algebraic_curve#Rational_curves NURBS exact circle, same weight form: Piegl & Tiller, The NURBS Book, §7.4 (unverified page number).

Integer lattice. Yes, at the level of the circle, not the curve: the triples give whole-number points on the circle. Scaling the whole construction by q²+p² makes the circle integer at the cost of a growing grid — which is the grid-bill question in state/ACHIEVEMENTS-int.md, not a new one.

8. Pythagorean-hodograph (PH) curves — the polynomial analogue

Definition. A polynomial curve whose derivative has rational length, so arc length is a polynomial; equivalently, a curve whose hodograph is a Pythagorean triple of polynomials.

Citations. Wikipedia, "Pythagorean hodograph curve" — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythagorean_hodograph_curve R. T. Farouki, "Introduction to Pythagorean-hodograph curves" — https://faculty.engineering.ucdavis.edu/farouki/wp-content/uploads/sites/51/2021/07/Introduction-to-PH-curves.pdf R. T. Farouki, Pythagorean-Hodograph Curves: Algebra and Geometry Inseparable (Springer, 2008).

Integer lattice. Closest existing exactness programme: PH curves are polynomial, so integer control points stay integer under the Bernstein form. But PH is about the tangent, not about a circle parameter — no trig, and the curve does not re-derive from an angle.

Verdict for curve-lab

The literature has a name for each half of our construction, and no name for the whole of it:

  • "circle-driven parameter" → circular harmonics / Fourier descriptors (shape analysis, real amplitudes, no exactness claim).
  • "trigonometric polynomial parametrisation" → trigonometric Bézier / Chebyshev-Lissajous (exact circles, real control points, no lattice).
  • "exact rational circle parameter" → stereographic / Pythagorean triples and PH curves (exact, integer-friendly, but about the circle or the tangent, never about a whole curve family on one grid).

No prior art found for the combination we use: an integer-lattice curve whose parameter steps by rational points of a circle (p-triples), so the curve is simultaneously exact, integer, and angle-driven. The nearest neighbours are PH curves (exact, integer control net, no trig) and the stereographic circle parametrisation (exact, integer, angle-driven, but only for the circle itself).

That is the honest claim: the mechanism is classical (Ptolemy → Chebyshev → NURBS weights), the application to a grid family of algebraic curves appears to be ours. Say "no prior art found in a 2026-11-19 web search", not "novel".
