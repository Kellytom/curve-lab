The full ladder for the decision — every rung on one screen

The 3-colour print is in the set today; the operator asked for higher. Here is every rung measured from the kept originals, heaviest plate first. Note the refused rung: 8 colours + 90-99 does not exist — pngquant declines it (exit 99) because 8 colours cannot meet that quality window; the honest rungs near it are 16 colours + 90-99 (worst 13–16, about the same size as plain 70-95) and plain 90-99 (worst 6–8, biggest files). More curves at the floor — the wash-out check

The operator's report: at the floor rung the greys get washed out. Here are eight more curves, original on the left, floor (quality 0-10) on the right, so the wash-out can be judged curve by curve. The numbers to read are the moved share and the grey count: c-0091 dithers 18.4% of its pixels and c-0022 13.9% — those are where "washed out" should show. One oddity, stated plainly: c-0088's floor output is bigger than its original (71,168 vs 16,481 bytes) — the dither noise on that plate costs more than the palette saves, so the floor is not even a win there. The floor — worst quality, as requested

Three rungs below "bad". 0–10 keeps 4–5 grey levels (the survivor of the family: only 3–4% of pixels move, worst 60/255). 3 colours lets the dithering spread, and on the heavy plate it dithers 78% of the image — grainy everywhere. 2 colours is pure black and white, no grey at all: every pixel in the plate changes (mean 8–13 steps), and it is not even smaller on the dense plate, because dither noise is expensive to store. Click to zoom; the 2-colour rows are where the antialiasing has been deleted, not compressed.

How far can we squeeze? — the ladder, worst first

You asked for the bottom of the ladder, so here it is above the good ones. bad is --quality 5-25 (6 grey levels left): edges step up to 50/255 — that is a visible stair on the curves. almost bad is --quality 35-55 (8 levels): steps of 27–28/255, right at the edge of what a printed page shows.
