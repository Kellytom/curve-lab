# Prior art — curves, ornaments, and the human figure (searched 2026-11-23)

*What exists in the literature for our equations and for a sacred figure at the centre. Sources at the bottom. This is measurement of the field, not a design.*

## 1. PH curves used for ornament — published, and close

**Klár & Valasek, "Employing Pythagorean Hodograph curves for artistic patterns" (Acta Cybernetica 20(1), 2011).** A design tool where the artist places a hierarchy of **control circles**; PH **quintic spiral** segments join them at G2 or better, producing "vines, swirls, swooshes and floral components" — exported as **cubic Béziers**. This is the closest published work to our ornament use. Two honest differences:

- theirs is float CAD geometry; ours is an exact rational grid with integer rows, proved grid/bend/rails (measure.py), and a cubic-span conversion with stated errors;
- theirs joins circles; ours builds from recipes whose degree is read from the name.

So **PH-for-ornament is prior art** (2011). The exact grid and the proofs are what we must claim — and must not overstate anything else.

**Farouki, *Pythagorean-Hodograph Curves: Algebra and Geometry Inseparable* (Springer 2008)** and **Farouki & Sakkalis (1990)** are the field's foundations; the IBM J. Res. Dev. paper ("Pythagorean hodographs") is the classic introduction. Offset curves, arc length, CNC paths, motion control — nothing about ornaments there.

## 2. The human figure on a grid — the precedent is ancient and precise

- **The Egyptian canon**: the standing figure laid out on a grid of 18 (later 19) squares, every landmark on a square boundary — the oldest codified "figure on a measured grid". Directly analogous to our grid-first approach.
- **Polyclitus' canon** (Greek): proportional rules for the ideal body; **Dürer's *Four Books on Measurement*** (1525): human proportions constructed by geometric schemes.
- **Byzantine iconography** has its own canon: the **Winfield & Winfield monograph** (*Proportion and Structure of the Human Figure in Byzantine Wall-Painting and Mosaic*, 1982) and the painters' **Hermeneia** tradition give landmark proportions for the sacred figure — the exact tradition the operator named (the Virgin, Christ, the hearts).
- **Sacred Heart / Immaculate Heart iconography** (Britannica; christianiconography.info; Encyclopedia.com): a codified set of geometric elements — heart, crown of thorns (a closed ornament), rays, cross above, the wound; the Immaculate Heart adds roses, lilies and a sword. Every one of those is an ornament primitive the existing fleur/petal grammar can build.

## 3. Rendering a face in line — two published routes

- **Ostromoukhov, "Digital Facial Engraving" (SIGGRAPH '99).** The classical insight: engraving lines **follow the facial form** — layers of hatching along the features, merged by rules. It is tone in lines, not an outline. Our offset/parallel machinery (the same exact-fraction rails) can generate curvature-following hatching.
- **Outline portraits in Béziers**: PortraVec / VectorPD (2024) fit Bézier strokes to facial features from images. The outline route, automated.

So the figure has two honest constructions: **silhouette/outline** (traced, fitted) or **engraved tone** (hatching along form). The second is closer to the book's world and to Ostromoukhov's rules.

## 4. Tracing a reference into splines — the workflow has a name

- **Ikarus** (URW, 1970s–80s) digitized typefaces by placing spline points on scanned drawings — the historical precedent for "measured reference → fitted spans".
- **FontForge / potrace / autotrace**: raster → outline splines, automatic.
- **Spiro (Levien) / Hobby's Metafont splines**: minimum-curvature splines for type design; **Levien's thesis** *From Spiral to Spline* is the standard treatment of clothoid splines in interactive design.

Nothing found does "fit spans to a statue/engraving then prove the result on an exact grid" — the measured-reference workflow is standard; the grid discipline is ours.

## 5. What was NOT found (and must not be claimed)

- No published sacred-figure construction on an exact rational grid with integer speed rows and proved measures.
- No published ornament system that reads its degree from a recipe name and refuses beyond a ceiling.
- The PH-for-ornament idea itself **is** published (2011) — any claim of novelty must be limited to the exactness, the grid and the proofs.

## 6. What the field suggests for the figure plan

1. **Choose the canon first** — Byzantine (Winfield) or Egyptian/Polyclitus. The figure is then constructed against landmarks, not drawn freehand.
2. **Decide outline or engraved tone.** Recommendation: outline for the silhouette and the hearts (fitting spans on the grid); engraved tone for the face (hatching along form, generated by the exact rails).
3. **The hearts are ornaments, not portraits** — ray, thorn-crown, rose and lily are geometry the existing grammar already makes; the Sacred Heart and Immaculate Heart pair can be built before any face exists.
