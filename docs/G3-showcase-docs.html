What it is for

The destination is a curve that Inkscape or a glyph editor can open as ordinary Bézier nodes and edit without a smoothness surprise. That means:

  • four-point cubic spans where a cubic chain can carry the curve;
  • every join measured in position, tangent, curvature and curvature-rate;
  • the editable data stated: integer control points, handle lengths and the rational slope of each handle;
  • the join errors kept, but on the notes page rather than in the way of the showcase.

The showcase is not a gallery of pretty shapes. It is the handoff sheet and the proof that the handoff is smooth.

The continuity ladder and the gate

At a join, the gate measures four gaps:

gap what it is exact construction integer-rounded source
|Δp| position 1e-9 1e-5
angle tangent direction 1e-6° 1e-6°
|Δκ| curvature 1e-9 1e-5
|Δ(dκ/ds)| curvature-rate 1e-9 1e-5

The 1e-9 values are floating-point noise for exact constructions: a single cubic cut into pieces, a degree-elevated span, or a join solved algebraically. They are not a universal law. The recovered LAC page has integer-rounded control points, so its joins are G3 only to the data's precision. Across the 31 recovered LAC chains the measured residual runs from 5.6e-8 to 7.96e-6; LAC Fig. 6 is at 5.6e-8. Requiring 1e-9 there asks the data to be more precise than it is, and the old code responded by re-fitting a quintic. The source tolerance is therefore 1e-5, while exact constructions keep 1e-9. The gate is a declared per-source tolerance; the page still prints each measured gap. The gaps are separate: a source chain can have a tiny dκ/ds residual and still fail a 0.1° tangent gap or a curvature gap.

Two further checks matter:

  • Interior speed floor. A join-only test can pass two spans that each graze a near-cusp inside. A span is admitted only when its speed never falls below 10% of its chord. This is what keeps the dκ/ds graph readable and the "smooth" badge honest.
  • Fidelity. A curve is admitted only when the geometric deviation from the source is under 5% of the curve's own size. A source corner smoothed inside that tolerance is delivered smooth; the fit says so explicitly when it had to trim a singular endpoint.

Degree: cubic first

A curve goes to Inkscape or a glyph editor as four-point cubic spans. A cubic has four control points; meeting G3 at both ends asks for six conditions (tangent direction, curvature and curvature-rate at each end). A single cubic is therefore over-determined for a generic G3 join. A quintic span has six control points and two free handle lengths, which is exactly enough to meet the six conditions and still follow the source. That is why the quintic exists — and why it is a fallback, not a default.

The rule:

  1. Try a cubic chain first.
  2. Use a quintic span only when no cubic chain can meet the gate and the tolerance.
  3. The lead of the showcase carries only the few curves that need it — about five to ten — and each one states its specific reason.
  4. The same page carries the full gallery below, so one page gives the overall view. The quintic spans may appear in the side construction panels as the working view.
  5. The full fallback index may also exist as g3-handover.html.

The LAC correction

The recovered LAC spines are already cubic Bézier chains — six four-point spans each, with integer control points at the page scale. LAC Fig. 6 (p = 0, turn 270) is labelled S44 · Fig. 6 · LAC: p = 0, turn 270 in the showcase notes, not LAC · Fig. 6. Its joins measure G3 at 5.6e-8, which is the precision of the integer-rounded source. It is carried as its own cubic spans at the source tolerance, not re-fitted as a quintic. The S-number is 38 + figure number: Fig. 1 is S39, Fig. 6 is S44.

The other recovered LAC chains are not G3: their spans meet in position, but their tangent gaps run up to 0.19° and their curvature gaps up to 1.3e-4. They are rejected with those measured reasons; they are not silently re-fitted as quintics. Finding a cubic G3 construction for those chains is the open cubic-first task, and the diagnostics page shows where the joins fail.

The page: what is where

The main showcase is one page:

  1. Cubic lead — six panels, every span four control points. Five are one cubic cut into five pieces; the bell is eight independent cubic spans. The joins are G3 by identity or by solve, and the panel states its speed floor.
  2. Quintic-required — the five curves above, each with the specific reason and the six-point spans shown in the side construction.
  3. Cusp family — the complete admitted set, plus rejected comparisons: - admitted: astroid, deltoid, nephroid, epicycloid k=3, epicycloid k=5, hypocycloid k=5, cardioid, heart · Beutel lower; - rejected: semicubical and pear, kept beside them with the measured reason.
  4. Deviation cases — squircle and heart · Beutel upper, not hidden.
  5. The full gallery — every G3-complete curve, grouped by source, curve left and dκ/ds right. This is where the overall view lives.

Figures are numbered consecutively from 1. The gallery follows the lead; the counts are generated below.

Each gallery panel shows:

  • the ideal curve dashed, the delivered G3 chain solid, the joins circled;
  • dκ/ds against arc length, with green joins and gold declared breaks. The gallery carries no tight ring; a tight span is ringed in gold on the diagnostics page only;
  • a record: branches, spans, deviation as a share of size, worst Δ(dκ/ds), native degree, speed floor, and any declared feature;
  • a zoom button that enlarges the panel and lets you scroll to the mark.

A tight span is below 25% of the chord speed; the 10% floor is the admission line. The diagnostics page collects the advisory rings in gold. Red is reserved for a rejected cusp or a failing join: a declared cusp is never a red ring. The figure-by-figure fixes and their proofs are tracked in state/G3-FIXES-int.md.

The handoff table

The six cubic lead panels carry the data the editor needs, in a table under the figure:

  • control points — integers at the stated scale (×625 for the integer panels), or real for the bell;
  • handle lengths — an integer when the squared length is a perfect square, otherwise the exact √n;
  • handle slopes — reduced fractions of the integer handle vector, or vertical.

The bell is honestly marked real, not integer. The quintic-required rows are not handoff rows and do not get the table; their side construction shows the six-point spans as the working view.

The error tables

The join error tables moved to workbench/g3-notes.html. The notes page repeats each of the six cubic curves with:

  • |Δp|, tangent angle, |Δκ|, |Δ(dκ/ds)|;
  • the pass/fail on every body join.

The showcase therefore carries the editable data, and the notes carry the measurement. The gate is computed from Bézier derivatives, never from the sampled polyline.

Every figure links to its own entry on the notes page (g3-notes.html#note-<curve>). The entry carries the measurement, the fix note, the join table and a back-link to g3-showcase.html#fig-<n>. A curve that appears both in the lead and in the gallery shares one note entry.

The fit and the cache

The catalogue attempts every candidate in scripts/g3_catalogue.py:

  • exact curves are read from their homogeneous control polygons;
  • sampled curves are read through a local cubic fit of their polyline;
  • a branch that cannot be interpolated G3 — a cusp, a point at infinity, a singular handle solve, or a deviation over 5% — is rejected and named.

The span solve (quintic_g3 in scripts/g3_showcase.py) fixes the endpoints and tangent directions, solves the two along-tangent components for the curvature-rate at both ends, and grids the two handle lengths for the smallest sampled deviation. The speed floor rejects a span that nearly stops inside.

The fit is cached at workbench/g3-catalogue-cache.json (gitignored, about 2 MB), keyed by FIT_VERSION plus a hash of the fit sources. The first run after a fit change takes about 3.4 minutes; later page rebuilds read the cache in about 3.6 seconds. Bump FIT_VERSION when the span solve or the speed gate changes.

Reproduce it

From the repository root:

# 1. Fit every candidate, write the catalogue page/JSON and the cache.
.venv/bin/python scripts/g3_catalogue.py

# 2. Build the one-page showcase and the notes (cache hit after step 1).
.venv/bin/python scripts/g3_showcase.py

# 3. Build the standalone gallery and the tightest-span diagnostics.
.venv/bin/python scripts/g3_handover.py

# 4. Refresh this document's counts and HTML twin.
.venv/bin/python scripts/g3_showcase_doc.py

# 5. Add the page bars and check the claims.
.venv/bin/python scripts/hub.py
.venv/bin/python -m unittest discover tests
.venv/bin/python scripts/recheck.py

What is still open

  • The cubic-first fitter is not universal yet. The LAC source is corrected and the five quintic-required curves are labelled with reasons, but the catalogue still has a quintic carrier for curves that are not cubic chains. A general cubic-span attempt that records its own failure is the next code task.
  • The other LAC chains are G1, not G3. Fig. 6 (S44) is the genuine cubic G3 chain; the rest of the recovered LAC spans have tangent gaps up to 0.19° and curvature gaps up to 1.3e-4. A cubic G3 construction for them is open.
  • The 1e-5 source tolerance is declared, not universal. Exact constructions keep 1e-9; a measured source gets the precision of its data. The page prints the measured gap either way.
  • Smooth is a subset. The gallery includes curves complete with a declared cusp or node. The smooth-handover count is the list to give an editor without a caveat.
  • The page is about 5 MB. The cache makes it cheap to generate, not necessarily cheap to open. Lazy-loading the gallery by group is the next presentation improvement.
  • No push or deploy is implied. The local page is the authority; the public URL stays private.

Keeping this document current

The Markdown is the source; the HTML twin is generated, never hand-edited.

  1. Edit docs/G3-showcase-docs.md (prose and structure).
  2. Run .venv/bin/python scripts/g3_showcase_doc.py. - It fills the counts block from the catalogue JSON and the showcase page. - It stamps the updated line with the date and commit. - It writes docs/G3-showcase-docs.html and embeds the Markdown's SHA.
  3. Run python -m unittest tests.test_g3_showcase_doc; it fails if the HTML twin or the counts are stale.

When the showcase changes, run the page generators first, then the doc generator, then the hub. Do not edit docs/G3-showcase-docs.html by hand.
