Stage II: plates

A spine is a chain of chords. Everything drawn from it — an offset, a branch — is a plate, and every plate is a construction on the spine, not a copy of it.

Offset parallel curves

Slide every chord by some distance d along its normal, then re-intersect the neighbours. The node angles do not change — a spine turns by 4.35° at every node, and so does every offset of it. What changes is the chord length: each end is trimmed by d·tan(α/2), so a chord of length c becomes c − 2d·tan(α/2).

That subtraction is the whole story. It is a constant, not a ratio, so an offset is not a scaled copy of the spine: the short chords at the hook lose proportionally far more than the long ones at the arm, which means every offset has its own b. Push far enough and the innermost chord is eaten to nothing — at d = c/(2·tan(α/2)) = 41146 the plate closes at a point. That number is the hook's own radius of curvature, c/α = 41166: the plate closes when the offset reaches the centre it is turning about.

The spine is b = 1.62. Offsetting inward raises b and outward lowers it, which is why a plate family reads as one gesture at different tensions.

Where the cubics fit

A span is a cubic through two nodes. The repo fits it with the parabola rule — arm = chord / (3·cos²(δ/2)), exact for a circular arc — and groups the chords so no span carries more than about 45°, which is how 9 cubics stand in for 45 chords. Nodes are smoothed onto the bisector, so consecutive spans meet G1 and the chain stops reading as a polygon.

But a cubic does not survive offsetting. The exact offset of a cubic is a degree-10 curve, not a cubic. The usual shortcut — slide the four control points along their normals — is an approximation: it lands 4% of the offset distance away from a plate fitted properly, and that share does not shrink as the offset gets smaller. It is a shape error, not a rounding error.

9 spans on the spine (ink) · the plate at d = 12344, spans fitted to the offset spine (dark) · the same plate by moving the four control points (gold) · worst gap 4% of the offset, on span 1 of 9

So the order is fixed: offset the spine first, then fit spans to the plate that comes out. Offsetting the cubics and hoping is the one move this geometry punishes.

Root and branch

The root is a spine. A branch is another spine, scaled down and started at a node along that node's own tangent — which is the incoming chord direction, so the join is G1 for free: there is no blend to compute and no corner to round. The child then turns by the same angle at every node, only faster, because its chords are shorter.

Where a branch may go is the branch-points question — clearance from the rest of the study. This is how it grows.

Same spine, same child, three rules — and the difference between a fern and a fishbone is only whether the side flips. Depth is the next dial: each child is a spine too, so it branches as well.
