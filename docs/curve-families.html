Spirals that make a dress curve

Log spirals, hyperbolic spirals and a clothoid, all at the same scale. Which bend does the hip curve have?

Logarithmic spirals: r = a e^(bθ)

The tangent makes a constant angle with the radius. Small b is almost a circle; large b opens fast. A hip curve is a gentle arc, so it would sit at the small end.

Hyperbolic spirals: r = a / θ

The radius falls as 1/θ. It has a horizontal asymptote at y = a and winds into the pole; the curves below are different windows on the same spiral.

Clothoid, for comparison

clothoid · 90°
κ = s / A²
curvature linear in arc length

The clothoid's curvature is linear in arc length; the log spiral's radius of curvature is proportional to the radius; the hyperbolic has rθ constant. Those are three different signatures.

How to tell which one you have

familyequationsignaturefit
log spiralr = a e^(bθ) tangent–radius angle constant; ρ ∝ s log ρ vs log s has slope +1; b = tan(angle)
clothoidκ = s / A² curvature linear in arc length; ρ ∝ 1/s log ρ vs log s has slope −1
hyperbolicr = a / θ rθ constant; asymptote at y = a fit rθ; the asymptote gives a
log-aestheticρ^α = c₀s + c₁ α = 1 is log, α = −1 is clothoid fit α; the slope of log ρ vs log s is 1/α

If the hip curve is one of these, the fitted numbers will say which; if it is a compound (as the vary form often is), it will fit two segments with different α or b.
