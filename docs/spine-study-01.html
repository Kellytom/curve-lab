What the chains say

Each span keeps its own law: a line is zero turns, an arc is a constant turn, the Euler span opens 0, 1, 2, … turns. The chords are exact rational lengths on exact rational headings, so every node is rational before the sheet places it. The engine receives the chain as straight segments with the chain's own corner at each node — one geometry kind, no second kernel.

The strokes are drawn as centre lines on purpose. A ribbon width would ask the engine's rail builder to offset a chain that turns a degree at every node; those rails cross at the corners, because they are built for smooth clothoids. The filled ribbon is the spine layer's miter job (ribbon.outline), and that is the next connection to make.

The drift is the price of the group: the start heading is snapped to the nearest degree and every corner with it, so each quantised corner swings the rest of the stroke on a long lever. The table is the honest measurement, not an error to be hidden. Finer turns do not converge in practice: 0.1° did not finish in two minutes in whole-number arithmetic.
