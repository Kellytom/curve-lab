Bounding parabola

A y-monotonic cubic, its area-fitted parabola, the two hybrid quadratics that bracket it, and the resulting strip. Drag the ends in x and the handles freely. Positive y is up in the math; the canvas is y-down and flipped once at draw.

drag ○ ends · □ handles

What is happening, in plain language

  1. Make the curve one-dimensional. The cubic is y-monotonic: one x for every y. Write it as x = f(y).
  2. Replace it with a parabola. Fit x = c₀ + c₁y + c₂y² by matching the two ends and the signed area between the cubic and its chord. It is a stand-in, not the curve.
  3. Bracket the stand-in. Two hybrid quadratics are built from the handles. They sandwich the cubic. Sampling their distance to the fitted parabola gives a minimum and maximum, so the cubic must lie inside the strip between p(y)+dmin and p(y)+dmax.
  4. Use the strip for intersection. For two curves, compare their strips. If the strips do not overlap, the curves cannot intersect there. If they do overlap, subdivide the y-range and repeat. That is the “cubic scaling” claim.

The strip shown here is sampled for the demo. The production prototype uses cubic root solving/tangency for tighter bounds; it also handles cusps, near-coincident curves, and Fréchet-distance fallbacks.

What it is not

  • Not an offset builder. It brackets intersections of two curves; it does not construct a rail at distance d.
  • Not an integer-lattice method. Integer endpoints/handles do not make the parabola bound or the cubic integer.
  • Not the Levien fit. Levien fits an offset rail. This fits a sideways parabola to a cubic so an intersection can be bracketed.
  • Not production-ready. The public prototype is a draft branch; it needs y-monotonic preprocessing, cusp handling, robust root finding, and a usable API.

Why it is not our next move

Our current problem is offsets, endings, and ornament grammar. The bounding-parabola method solves a different primitive: robust curve/curve intersection for boolean path ops. It would add a large numerical subsystem without producing a rail, a terminal, or a lattice hit. Keep it in the “revisit if we need boolean ops or robust crossing/clearance” box.
