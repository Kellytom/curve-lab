Cruciform proof 01

Two full lines crossing, built as two declared junctions and one trunk with section stations — not as an undeclared X.

The cross. The trunk runs vertically at width 0.5; the two arms leave it at width 0.3. Each arm is a `Junction`: a branch, a circular fillet of radius 0.6, and a declared merge. The join is G1, with a curvature step of 1.6667; the page states that rather than calling it G2.

The sections. The dashed bars are `Section` stations across the trunk — spine, tangent, width and both rails at one arc length. They are the authoring view: the rails are derived from the section, not measured from a drawing.

The endings. All four tips end in cone terminals of R = 2 × offset. The arms are not bare tips, and the crossing is legal only because both merges are declared.

The three aspects

Linearity: the trunk and the two arms are straight runs. Curve: each fillet is a circular arc where the bend is the point. Infinity: absent — the cross is bounded. The proof is a legal component, not a complete design: pair it with a spiral tail or a ray to satisfy the third aspect.

Grounded: the horizontal arms are the datum; the trunk is the vertical. The cross stands on its arms.

Why it is a proof

A cruciform is not a cross drawn twice: it is a junction problem. Each arm declares where it meets the trunk, the fillet is measured, the join grade is reported, and the four tips use the same terminal vocabulary as the stalks. The section object supplies the rails at a station, so the width is a number, not a stroke setting.
