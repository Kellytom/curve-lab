curve-lab · biarcs

Two arcs, one G1 join

A biarc is the simplest curve that matches two points and two tangents: two circular arcs that meet with a common tangent. This page builds the equal-radius member — both arcs get the same R, so the centres are P ± R·n and the arcs touch when |C₀C₁| = 2R. That is one quadratic in R. The joint is the midpoint of the centres; the join is G1 by construction, not by fitting.

Fig. B1 · three biarcs

Red: the joint. Blue: the two centres. The measured join is the angle between the directed tangents there, and it is zero to rounding: e 0.414 R / 0.0e+00°, e 1.544 R / 1.2e-06°, a 1.010 R / 0.0e+00°.

What it is and is not

Is: exact endpoints, exact end tangents, G1 at the joint, and arcs — so the offset of each piece is another arc, exactly. That is why biarcs matter for a width: an arc spline can carry a constant band with no fitting error.

Is not: exact in R. The equal-radius root is generally irrational (the first case is √2 − 1), so the centres are computed numbers. A biarc is an arc-exact curve, not a rational one, and it does not claim to be a NAMED curve.

For the lattice the useful version is different: choose rational centres and radii on the grid, which constrains the endpoint data. The arches page does the mirroring; this page does the join.
