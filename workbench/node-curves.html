Which of these are LACs, and what the rest are built by

Most of these are not log-aesthetic curves, and they should not be built as if they were. The LAC law is rho^p = c0 s + c1: the radius of curvature as a power of a straight line in arc length. It holds exactly for the circle, the circle involute, the Euler spiral and the log spiral, and for nothing else on this page.

Each row carries the rule the curve is actually built by. The rolling-circle family and the roses are circle arcs joined at their cusps and nodes; the lemniscates and the piriform are algebraic curves with a node; the catenary is cosh; the Pell curve is a conic; the biarc and the Reuleaux triangle are circle arcs with tangent joins. Those are the rules to use for them.

The LAC column is kept because it was asked for, and because it shows one useful thing: a lobe trimmed away from its node is nearly a power law (the quadrifolium 0.015, the strophoid 0.020), so a lobe can be one LAC span with a declared join at the node. That is a construction choice, not a claim that the curve is an LAC.
