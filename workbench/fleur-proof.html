Fleur proof 01

The fleur study read through the plant-form grammar: stalk, two rails, an ending, integer marks, and the petal as a branch.

The reading. The central lance is the stalk. The study's 25-unit width is the pair of rails at ±12.5. The lance tip is no longer a bare point: it ends in a cone terminal of radius 2 × offset = 25. The side petal is the branch, and it ends in seagrass, because a branch inherits the ending rule. The gold dots are the integer nodes; two of them carry pyramid growth marks on the rails.

Nothing is redrawn by eye: the half, the mirror and the band come from curve_lab.grammar.study_01, and the counts below are recomputed from that module by scripts/fleur_proof.py.

fleur centreline +offset rail −offset rail integer node / growth mark seagrass branch ending

The ending, enlarged

The cone is two arcs of radius 25, one from each rail, each tangent at its base and meeting at the centreline. It is the rule for a stalk with no flower head, and it is what makes the lance tip legal. No bare tip.

R = 2 × offset · tangent at the rail base · one meeting point

What this proves

That the fleur study is not a special drawing: it is a plant-form with the same vocabulary as the LAC stalks and the integer spines. The central lance is a stalk, its rails are offsets, its tip is a terminal, its side petal is a branch with an ending, and its exact nodes are lattice points. The grammar holds on the ornament that was the acceptance test for the port.
