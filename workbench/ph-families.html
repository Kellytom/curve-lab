PH families · degree by degree

Every planar PH curve is z'(t) = h(t)·(u²−v², 2uv), and Farouki–Sakkalis fixes the degree: n = deg h + 2·max(deg u, deg v) + 1. With h=1 only odd degrees appear; the h factor is the only route to an even degree. Below are the built rungs, the non-PH integer cubic for contrast, and what is not built yet. Model coordinates, +y up; blue dashed lines are x=0/y=0.

Degree ladder. h=1, u,v linear → 3 · h linear, u,v linear → 4 · h=1, u,v quadratic → 5 · h quadratic, u,v linear → 5 · h linear, u,v quadratic → 6 · h=1, u,v cubic → 7. The rational h gives the rational PH family (the circle).

n = 3 · Tschirnhausen PH cubic PH

h=(1) · u=(2,−2) · v=(1,−2) · scale 24. Every proper cubic PH curve is the Tschirnhausen cubic up to scale/rotation: one shape, no inflection, a C-curve. The cubic interpolant in hodograph.py is a segment of this shape.

n = 3 · integer cubic 3-4-5 not PH

An exact integer-control cubic, not a PH curve. The control triple is Pythagorean, but the hodograph is not: the 3-4-5 base has no rational offset. This is the pyth_cubic family; see integer cubics.

n = 4 · PH quartic PH

h=(1,2) · u=(1,−1) · v=(1,−2) · scale 1536. The even degree needs deg h = 1. It can inflect, which the cubic cannot.

n = 5 · PH quintic 1 · u, v quadratic PH

h=(1,) · u=(1, 2, 1) · v=(1, -1, 1) · scale 81,920 · arc 69/10 rational. One of three representative cases in ph_quintic.py; the general quintic family is not enumerated.

n = 5 · PH quintic 2 · u, v quadratic PH

h=(1,) · u=(1, 0, 1) · v=(0, 2) · scale 491,520 · arc 16/5 rational. One of three representative cases in ph_quintic.py; the general quintic family is not enumerated.

n = 5 · PH quintic 3 · h quadratic, u, v linear PH

h=(1, 1, 1) · u=(2, 1) · v=(1, -1) · scale 81,920 · arc 129/10 rational. One of three representative cases in ph_quintic.py; the general quintic family is not enumerated.

n = 6 · PH sextic PH

h=(1,2) · u=(−1,1,0) · v=(2,−1,0) · scale 240. Even degree, with quadratic generators: the multiple-cusp shapes live here.

n = 7 · PH septic PH

u=(1,2,1,1) · v=(1,−1,1,0) · h=(1) · scale 1,720,320. The degree-7 integer example; the general septic family is not built.

n = 2 rational · the circle PH

x=(1−t²)/(1+t²), y=2t/(1+t²); speed 2/(1+t²) is rational, so the arc length and offset are rational. There is no polynomial PH n=2; the rational circle is the n=2 rung.

What is made, and what is not

Made: the Tschirnhausen cubic; the quartic and sextic examples with h; three representative PH quintics; the degree-7 integer PH; the rational circle; PH cubic interpolation (hodograph.py, catenary.html); the integer scalings of each.

Not made yet: the general PH quintic family — ph_quintic.py has three examples, not the five-parameter family; the PH quintic Hermite interpolant and C² PH spline; the PH quintic transition curve (line to arc); the general degree-7 family (one example); the n=8+ ladder; and rational PH beyond the circle.

The pyth_cubic integer family is exact but not PH: its name is the control triple, not the hodograph. The cubic PH family is the Tschirnhausen, one shape. The PH-INTEGER notes have the arithmetic; the missing families have the rational circle and the septic; the catenary is four PH cubics; the quintic G3 page is a different (interpolation) story.

Plain English

A PH curve is a curve whose speed is a polynomial. That one condition is the whole subject. It makes the arc length exact (a polynomial integrates to a polynomial), the tangent direction come from a rational formula, and the offset curve stay rational instead of being refitted point by point.

The degree ladder is bookkeeping. The hodograph has an extra factor h and two polynomial parts u and v. With h = 1 and linear u, v the curve is cubic; a linear h gives a quartic; quadratic u, v give a quintic; and so on. Odd degrees come from h = 1; the h factor is the only route to an even degree.

The integer lattice hits. A hit is a point at a chosen rational parameter that lands on integer coordinates after one common scale. The point is not a new curve: it is a choice of parameter value and unit. A larger grid (a larger m) gives more hits, but the common scale grows too. That trade is the whole story of the integer tables. This page does not draw the dots yet; the next version overlays them.

PHH and PPH (2D only). The only self-imposed limitation is to stay in the plane. PHH means PH Hermite: a PH curve through two points with prescribed tangents, the PH quintic Hermite interpolant. PPH means piecewise PH: PH spans joined with continuity, the C² PH spline. The spatial/helix reading is out. Neither should be assumed to be plain PH.

The trap. The pyth_cubic integer cubic is exact but not PH: its name is the control triple, not the hodograph. Every proper cubic PH curve is the Tschirnhausen cubic, up to scale and rotation. The familiar nodal alpha has a self-crossing and its endings on the right; the cubic card here currently shows one C-shaped arc of that same alpha, and the next version adds the full nodal curve.

What sliders would add. Sliders help only if they expose the constraints. The useful set is the PH-valid generators, the grid m with a live hit count and common scale, the four quadrants and mirror, and the offset d showing the exact PH offset beside a fitted non-PH offset. A “break PH” toggle makes the difference visible. The PH Hermite and piecewise modes need their own controls (endpoint tangents, join continuity), not the same row.
