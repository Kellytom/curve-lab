A PH quintic that lands on a coarse lattice

TQ(15) is a primitive, polynomial Pythagorean-hodograph quintic with a constant second hodograph component. Both coordinates are whole numbers at every integer parameter, the control points are integers on a scale-5 grid, and the length between stops is rational. So the curve can be cut at its lattice points without losing exactness.

The family

Write the hodograph as w = k(t²−1) + i. Then x′ = k²(t²−1)² − 1 and y′ = 2k(t²−1), and the speed is σ = k²(t²−1)² + 1 — a polynomial, so the curve is PH and its length is exact. The degree is 2·2 + 1 = 5. h = 1 makes it polynomial; gcd(u, v) = 1 makes it primitive; v constant makes it Tschirnhausen type — the degree-3 member of exactly this family is the Tschirnhausen cubic.

Because v is constant, y′ is quadratic and y is a cubic — the lowest degree a quintic allows. Only x carries the fifth-degree work.

Where it hits whole numbers

The ÷5 in x comes from integrating t⁴; the ÷3 in y comes from integrating t². Whichever divides k disappears, and the hit period follows. The scale never exceeds 15, so there is no ceiling on k — only the control numbers grow, like k².

Cutting at a stop

Take the piece t ∈ [0, 1]. Reparametrised to u ∈ [0, 1] it is a quintic Bézier with integer controls at scale 5:

x: (0, 224, 448, 597, 596, 595) · y: (0, −30, −60, −85, −100, −100)

The same holds for every unit piece t ∈ [m, m+1]: shifting t = u + m keeps integer coefficients, so the controls stay integers at scale 5, and the piece is still PH. Cut at t = 1, 2, 3 … and each piece has an exact endpoint and an exact length. That is the difference from the Tschirnhausen cubic: there, exact lattice nodes meant irrational chord sides; here the cut points and the lengths are both exact.

Why not the midpoint chain

The triangle chain still produces whole-number steps on any PH curve, but a quintic's speed is quartic, so the chain's grid grows like the fourth power of the step denominator. On TQ(3) over t ∈ [0, 5] with twelve steps the chain needs a grid of 442,368 and still misses the endpoint by 31 units in x; the cubic's 256 and 1/64 were a degree-3 accident. For a quintic, cut at the exact stops instead.
