A PH quintic that lands on a coarse lattice

TQ(75) is a primitive, polynomial Pythagorean-hodograph quintic with a constant second hodograph component. Every integer t is a whole-number point, the control points are integers on a grid of 5 cells per unit, and the length between stops is rational. So the curve can be cut at its lattice points without losing exactness.

The family

Write the hodograph as w = k(t²−1) + i. Then x′ = k²(t²−1)² − 1, y′ = 2k(t²−1), and the speed is σ = k²(t²−1)² + 1 — a polynomial, so the curve is PH and its length is exact. The degree is 2·2 + 1 = 5. h = 1 makes it polynomial; gcd(u, v) = 1 makes it primitive; v constant makes it Tschirnhausen type — the degree-3 member of exactly this family is the Tschirnhausen cubic. Because v is constant, y is a cubic; only x carries the fifth-degree work.

k stretches the same shape: x grows like k², y like k. TQ(3) is compact enough to read; TQ(75) is the same curve on a much longer leash. k is taken positive: a negative k is the same curve mirrored in the x-axis — the hits, the grid and the lengths do not change — and k = 0 degenerates to a straight line.

How many hits, and how fine the grid

Whole-number points come at integer t. How often, and how fine the grid, depends only on which of 3 and 5 divide k. In the window t = 0…5:

k = 75 is the same class as k = 15: every integer t, grid 5. Raising k does not buy more hits — it only multiplies the coordinates by (k/15)². The biggest control number is the largest number in the six control points of the first piece.

Fig. Q2 · all four quadrants

The curve is odd in both coordinates, so the four quadrants hold two mirror copies of one shape. The stops at t = ±2, ±3 are ringed; the origin and its S are the next figure.

Fig. Q3 · the S, the inflection, the turn

At t = 0 the curvature is zero: the origin is an inflection. Near t = ±1 the x-speed nearly vanishes, so the curve turns back on itself — the small wiggle. The dashed rings are the points where y = 0, at t = ±√3, which are not rational stops.

Fig. Q4 · cutting at the stops

Three pieces, cut at t = 1 and t = 2. Each is a PH quintic with integer endpoints and a rational length; the pieces can be separated and used on their own.

Cutting, in controls

Take the piece t ∈ [0, 1]. Reparametrised to u ∈ [0, 1] it is a quintic Bézier with integer controls on the grid of 5:

x: (0, 5624, 11248, 14997, 14996, 14995) · y: (0, -150, -300, -425, -500, -500)

The same holds for every unit piece t ∈ [m, m+1]: shifting t = u + m keeps integer coefficients, so the controls stay integers on the same grid, and the piece is still PH. That is the difference from the Tschirnhausen cubic: there, exact lattice nodes meant irrational chord sides; here the cut points and the lengths are both exact.

Fig. Q5 · what the two routes cost

The chain's whole-number steps are real, but at degree 5 they need a grid of 442,368 and still miss the endpoint by 31 units in x. Cutting at the exact stops needs only the points whole: a grid of 5.

Fig. Q6 · coordinate by coordinate

Both coordinates are integers at every integer t. That is the whole claim of the page, and it is what makes the stops usable.
