The generalized Archimedean spiral, and the radial of a LA curve

Gen-Arch is r = a + b·φ^(1/c). The dial c names the classical spirals: −2 lituus, −1 hyperbolic, 1 Archimedean, 2 Fermat. Its exact use is as the radial of a log-aesthetic curve: the centre of curvature of an LA curve traces a Gen-Arch spiral with c = α − 1 (Salvi 2026). Here that identity is measured, and the repo's own LAC dial is translated into it.

Fig. GA0 · what “radial” means

At each point of the clothoid, the osculating circle has radius ρ = 1/κ and a centre. The radial is those centres written as polar points about the origin: angle = the normal angle, radius = ρ. The left panel shows three; the right panel is all of them — the curve the centres trace is the lituus. That is the whole object: not an offset, not an evolute drawn in place, but the radius of curvature used as a polar distance.

Fig. GA1 · the four classical cases, a = 0, b = 1

φ runs 0.55–18 for the two negative-c spirals (they need φ > 0) and 0–18 for the positive ones. The lituus winds in from r = 1.35, the hyperbolic from 1.8; the Archimedean and Fermat wind out. Curvature is monotone where (t²+1)c² ≥ |c|√(c²+3).

Fig. GA2 · the radial identity

For each LA slope α, the radial is r = const·φ^(1/(α−1)); the log–log line is straight and the fitted exponent matches 1/(α−1) to six places. The constant is ((α−1)/α)^(1/(α−1)); the largest relative deviation over 241 samples is in the table.

What the dials mean, and what this corrects

Three different numbers are in play, and they are easy to confuse. α is the slope of the logarithmic curvature histogram — the paper's LA parameter, with κ ∝ s−1/α. p is this repo's LAC dial, the curvature exponent, with κ ∝ sp; therefore p = −1/α. c is the Gen-Arch dial, and for a radial c = α − 1 = −1/p − 1. So the showcase card labelled p = 3 is α = −1/3, not α = 3; and α = 3 would be p = −1/3, whose radial is the Fermat spiral. The label on the stem cuts now says p = 3, which is the repo's own dial and cannot be mistaken for α or for the p-triple's a.

Special cases: clothoid (α = −1, p = 1) → c = −2, the lituus; Nielsen's spiral (α = 0) → c = −1, the hyperbolic spiral; circle involute (α = 2, p = −1/2) → c = 1, the Archimedean. The logarithmic spiral (α = 1) is the exception: its radial is a logarithmic spiral, and c = 0 is the limit of Gen-Arch, not a member.

The practical consequence is the paper's Section 5: a Gen-Arch segment with c = α − 1 is a cheap approximant of the LA curve, matching curvature at the start and approaching the same LCH slope as t grows. The limitation is at small t, where the fit is short; at large t the two are hard to tell apart.
