48 PH quintics: 24 S and 24 C, on a 1/5 grid

Every curve here is r'(t) = (u^2 - v^2, 2uv) with u and v quadratic and coefficients on the 1/5 grid in +/-2. An S bends one way and then the other - the heading swings at least 15 deg on each side of the inflection. A C bends one way throughout. All are PH, so the offsets are exact and a span is G3-capable.

Fig. Q6b | the exact balanced family

Balance is not a search result here - it is forced by the choice of the two speed recipes:

u = a(t^2 - t) + 1        v = b(t^2 - t)

W = u v' - v u' = b(2t - 1)      zero at t = 1/2, and nowhere else

r(1/2 + h) + r(1/2 - h) = 2 r(1/2)      the curve is point-symmetric about its middle

The Wronskian is a straight line through t = 1/2, so the inflection is exactly there. The point symmetry means the tangent at t = 0 and at t = 1 are the same, and the swing on each side is exactly

swing per side = 2 | atan( b / (4 - a) )

So one number, b, sets the swing and a shifts where the bend sits. The whole family is a PH quintic, so the offsets are exact and the endpoints land on a small pitch (5 to 30 below).

Twelve members, a = 0, 1, 2 and b = 2...8: the swing runs from 53 deg to 152 deg per side, and every one is exactly balanced. This is the panel a slider would drive - b is the slider, and the top half is the bottom half turned through 180 deg.

Fig. Q7 | the sliders: b sets the size, morphLambda sets the bend

b sets the scale of the exact S. a shifts where the bend sits. morphLambda is the bend: it takes the exact family's speed recipes and adds a growing share of the simplified curve 15's, the two-lobe S. morphLambda = 0 is the exact S; morphLambda = 1 is the target. The starting recipe carries c = 1 - a free nudge: it adds nothing to the pitch and takes the bend from 1.09 to 1.15.

In plain words: bend is the arc length divided by the straight-line distance between the ends. A straight line is 1.00; the gentle exact S is about 1.09; the target is about 5.6. morphLambda does not scale the curve - it changes its shape, pulling the ends round into the two lobes. The target is the simplified curve 15: u = 0.2 - 2t + 2t^2, v = 0.4 - 1.5t + 1.6t^2, the clearest S of the six candidates tested.

In the code: b is scaleB, a is shiftA, morphLambda is morphLambda, the two recipes are speedX and speedY, and their far ends are targetSpeedX and targetSpeedY.

Tune it: v = k*u + c, with k = 4, c = -6 at the pick. k tilts the segment, c slides it toward or away from the origin; the readout follows both. This c is not Q7's c. Here it is the intercept of the line between the two recipes; in Q7 it is the constant term of v's own recipe, v = b(t^2 - t) + c, which was set to 1 to keep the pitch free. Fig. Q12 | the exact bend formula

No sampling is needed. Write the speed recipe in the centred frame h = t - 1/2 as w(h) = speedMid + slopeMid*h + curvatureMid*h^2, with speedMid = w(1/2), slopeMid = w'(1/2) and curvatureMid = w''(1/2)/2. Then, exactly:

arc   = |speedMid|^2 + (|slopeMid|^2 + 2 speedMid.curvatureMid)/12 + |curvatureMid|^2/80
chord = speedMid^2 + (2 speedMid*curvatureMid + slopeMid^2)/12 + curvatureMid^2/80
bend  = arc / |chord|

speedMid.curvatureMid is the dot product and speedMid*curvatureMid the complex product. These three lines reproduce every entry in the preset JSON to within 1e-4. The weights 1, 1/12, 1/80 are the integrals of 1, h^2, h^4 over [-1/2, 1/2] - they are the irregular ratios in the recipe, and nothing like Fibonacci is involved.

Why it bends. The chord is a sum of three complex vectors - speedMid^2, (2 speedMid*curvatureMid + slopeMid^2)/12, curvatureMid^2/80 - while the arc is a positive number, so the bend is large exactly when those three cancel. For the even pair (slopeMid = 0) the chord is speedMid^2 (1 + ratio/6 + ratio^2/80) with ratio = curvatureMid/speedMid, which vanishes at ratio = -20/3 +/- (8*sqrt(5)/3) i: |ratio| = 4*sqrt(5) ~ 8.94 at curvatureAngle = 138.19 deg. There the curve closes and the bend runs to infinity.

The method. Choose curvatureRatio = |curvatureMid|/|speedMid| and curvatureAngle = angle(speedMid, curvatureMid), read the bend off the chart, then pick integer vectors and clear the denominators. For bend 2 the level set is curvatureRatio ~ 6.2-7.0 for curvatureAngle between 115 deg and 150 deg; below about 113 deg no ratio reaches bend 2. The pick sits at curvatureRatio = 7.7, curvatureAngle = 114.6 deg. Because the formula is exact, the integer search is instant: over small integers the smallest bend-2 even pair is the pick itself, pitch 10, unique up to signs.

Above: bend against curvatureRatio for four curvatureAngle values, with the closing angle marked. The flat grey curve is 110 deg, where bend 2 cannot be reached at any ratio.

Each square root has two signs, so one diagram gives up to eight curves - mirrors and rotations of the same shape.

How to find a preset

Work with the speed path w(t) = u + i v, not with the curve. The curve is r = integral of w^2, so its heading is 2*arg(w): the bend is decided by how far the direction of w sweeps, and by how close w passes to the origin.

  1. Never let v be a multiple of u. If v = k*u then arg(w) is constant, the curve is a straight line (bend 1.00), and if that line passes the origin the speed vanishes and you get a cusp instead. Bend needs v/u to change with t.
  2. Choose the heading turn first. The heading is twice arg(w), so the turn is twice the sweep of the speed path. Measured on this page: turn 167 deg gives bend 2.00; 186 deg gives 2.0-2.7; 215 deg gives 5.58; past about 230 deg the curve loops and the bend runs away.
  3. Use the even pair u = speedXAtEnds - speedXCurvaturet + speedXCurvaturet^2, v = speedYAtEnds - speedYCurvaturet + speedYCurvaturet^2. Point symmetry puts the inflection exactly at t = 1/2 and makes the two lobes equal, so the turn is shared evenly.
  4. Fine-tune with the closest approach. Move the speed path nearer the origin to add bend at the same turn - but keep the closest approach strictly above zero, or the speed vanishes and the curve cusps.
  5. Clear the denominators last. The pitch is the lcm of the integrated coefficients' denominators; scaling the whole recipe by n divides the pitch by n^2. Decimals cost pitch - 1.5 is dearer than 2.
  6. Read three numbers before keeping it: bend, an inflection strictly inside (0, 1), and a closest approach above zero. No inflection means a C, not an S; closest 0 means a cusp.

Worked example: the even pair with speedXCurvature = speedYCurvature = 4, speedYAtEnds = 0.7 is bend 2.69 at pitch 200. Drop speedYAtEnds to 0.5 and the same turn gives bend 2.24 at pitch 120; double the recipe to u = 1 - t + t^2, v = 1 - 8t + 8t^2 and the pitch is 30, endpoint (7, -14). All fourteen saved recipes are in the preset JSON and on the preset sheet.

How the tests read

aspect is the bounding box, width / height. Requiring 0.5-2.0 keeps a curve from being a long thin strip that fills the panel as a nearly straight line.

min(s0, s1) >= 40 deg is the heading change on the shorter side of the inflection: s0 from t = 0 to the inflection, s1 from there to t = 1. Taking the smaller forces a visible bend on both sides - a C with a flat tail fails it.

Loops. Point symmetry means a loop on one side comes with a mirror loop on the other, so balanced loops arrive in pairs. A single loop would break the symmetry. A cusp at the middle is possible in principle but not in the exact family: W = b(2t-1) has a simple zero at 1/2, and a cusp needs a double zero. The morphLambda slider shows the other end: Q6's curve 15 is a nearly balanced loop, and its W is a general quadratic, not a line.

Moving the root inside

The S is the sign change of W = u v' - v u'. Adding a multiple of u to v does not change W - the Wronskian is blind to it - so you have to move v in a different direction. Take the operator's case 1, u = 1 + t/5 + t^2, v = t: then W = 1 - t^2, whose only root is at t = 1. Replace v by v = (4/9)t and W becomes (4/9)u - t u', which vanishes at t = 1/2. The curve turns from a C into an S, at the price of a 9 in the pitch.

That is the design lever in one sentence: choose where you want the turn, then choose v so that u v' - v u' vanishes there.
