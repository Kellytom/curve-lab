48 PH quintics: 24 S and 24 C, on a 1/5 grid

Every curve here is r′(t) = (u² − v², 2uv) with u and v quadratic and coefficients on the 1/5 grid in ±2. An S bends one way and then the other — the heading swings at least 15° on each side of the inflection. A C bends one way throughout. All are PH, so the offsets are exact and a span is G3-capable.

Fig. G1 · the grid, 8 by 6

Each thumbnail is drawn in its own box; the axes are the curve's own x = 0 and y = 0. The red ring is the inflection. The pitch under each panel is the grid that makes the coordinates whole at integer t — the price of the fractions.

Moving the root inside

The S is the sign change of W = u v′ − v u′. Adding a multiple of u to v does not change W — the Wronskian is blind to it — so you have to move v in a different direction. Take the operator's case 1, u = 1 + t/5 + t², v = t: then W = 1 − t², whose only root is at t = 1. Replace v by v = (4/9)t and W becomes (4/9)u − t u′, which vanishes at t = 1/2. The curve turns from a C into an S, at the price of a 9 in the pitch.

That is the design lever in one sentence: choose where you want the turn, then choose v so that u v′ − v u′ vanishes there.
