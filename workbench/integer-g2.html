Integer G2 mirror chain

The symmetric integer cubic has opposite endpoint curvatures. Reflecting it across an endpoint tangent gives a curve with the same tangent and curvature at the join. Scaling by the hypotenuse clears the reflection denominators. The result is an exact G2 chain with integer controls.

Fig. G1 · the 3-4-5 chain

The middle piece is the 3-4-5 base, scaled by 5. The pieces before and after are its mirrors at the start and end tangents. Both joins are C1 and G2; the outer ends are free.

Join data: tangent (15,−20); curvature +16/375 at the start join and −16/375 at the end join. The signs differ because the chain has an inflection between the joins.

What this proves, and what it does not

Proved: a three-piece integer-control chain with G2 at both interior joins exists for every symmetric triple in the catalogue. The mirror scale is the hypotenuse c, and the checks are exact rational equalities.

Not proved: that an arbitrary pair of integer cubics can be joined G2; that the chain is visually clear or free of self-intersection; or that the outer ends match a following piece. This probe uses mirrored copies of one symmetric family, which is a much narrower construction than the asymmetric-arm search.

The next probe should search asymmetric arm pairs and report the smallest integer scale that matches two chosen endpoint curvatures.
