Two chains, one step, one kink

Two integer stalks cannot simply be laid end to end and be both exact and smooth. The step between them is legal because 1/5 exists on both grids; the kink is what that step costs.

Fig. C1 · the composed stem

The two chains are the same family; only the placement differs. The link is a straight run, so it adds no curvature claim. Everything is exact — the step is a fraction, not a rounding.

What the seam costs

quantityvalueexact?
seam step0.200 = 1/5yes

tangent kink at the seam25.06°measured, not designed
position gap0yes, shared seam point

Position is exact and free; tangency is not. A step that exists on both grids still turns the stem, and that turn is the number to design with — either accept it as a node, or spend a connector that is tangent at both ends.

The cheap fix is a circular arc between the two ends: its radius must be a multiple of the coarser grid if its endpoints are to stay exact, which is the same trade one level down.
