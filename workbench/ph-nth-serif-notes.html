A serif foot as a PH appendix

The eleventh-power page ends a full line in a round arc for now. The parked idea is sharper: make the foot itself a PH span of the same degree, so flare, terminal and serif are one rational object rather than a rail, an arc and a drawing that agree by eye. This page keeps the code and the numbers so the problem can be picked up without carrying it on the sandbox.

The problem, stated

In the frame of the rail end — u along the rail's tangent, n the outward normal — the foot must run from (0, 0) to (h, f - d), leave along u, and arrive along the base (the n direction). That is four conditions: two positions and two directions. A speed of degree 5 gives twelve numbers, of which eleven are free after the scale is fixed, so the conditions leave a seven-parameter family. The page solves for one member by line search and prints how far it misses.

residual = |P(1) - target| + |tangent(0) - u| + |tangent(1) - base|

The flare, and the serif made from it

The chord terms of the default curve

One row per position power, x above y, on a shared zero line. These are the terms the integral produces for the sandbox's default curve, which is the curve the rest of this page works on. The first six rows are what a sextic can hold; the rest exist only because the position reaches t^11.

What the eleventh power costs

Every extra degree adds one speed pair and two position powers. The closure condition stays two equations, so the closed family grows by two dimensions per degree. That is more room for a shape, but it does not by itself give more loops: the wave families at degree 11, 13, 15 and 19 cross themselves zero times, and the loops this project has are degree 3 to 7. What the extra degrees buy is wiggles and sharper turns, not more crossings.

The price is the pitch: the integrated coefficients carry 1/(k+1) and the squares of the decimal denominators, so the integer scale climbs faster than the shape count. The low-pitch form of each saved curve is the same shape on a coarser grid; it is a change of units, not a new curve.

The flare is the reason for the sandbox: a serif is a width story, not a curvature story. The rail distance is a declared number, and the terminal radius is twice it, so flare, terminal and foot are one number apart rather than three drawings agreeing by eye.

Where it stands

Residuals on the sandbox's default curve were 0.019 to 0.025 at the dial settings tried, on a foot of height about 0.7 — two to three per cent. That is close enough to look at and not close enough to claim exact. The solver is a coordinate line search, not a proper Levenberg–Marquardt; the conditions are four and the unknowns eleven, so a least-squares solve with a numeric Jacobian should do better and is the first thing to try.

Two things are already exact and should not be lost: the rail is rational for a PH spine with a polynomial width law, and the arc terminal is a semicircle of radius d at that end. Only the foot is numerical.
