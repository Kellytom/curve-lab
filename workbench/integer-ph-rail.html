The integer PH curve and its exact rail

integer-105 is PH by construction, so its rail is a ratio of two polynomials — not a fitted cubic. The cubic fit is computed and printed as a number; nothing cubic is drawn, because cubics are the export step.

The source. The curve is written by the speed pair

u(t) = 2−5t + 6t²
v(t) = 4−20t + 20t²−2t³

and the curve is C(t) = ∫(u² − v², 2uv) dt, with C(0) = (0, 0). Both coordinates are polynomials:

x(t) = −12t + 70t²−511/3t³ + 189t⁴−444/5t⁵ + 40/3t⁶−4/7t⁷
y(t) = 16t−60t² + 328/3t³−112t⁴ + 52t⁵−4t⁶
σ(t) = u² + v² = 20−180t + 609t²−876t³ + 516t⁴−80t⁵ + 4t⁶

The pitch is 105: at that scale the eight position control points are whole numbers. It is the small grid the curve is written on, and it is not the grid its lattice points live on.

The rail is exact

The form. Because the speed σ is a polynomial, the unit normal is rational and the rail at signed distance d is

O(t) = C(t) + d·(−y'(t), x'(t)) / σ(t)

— a ratio of two exact polynomials, with no square root left in it. The page draws both rails at d = 41/1120 = 0.0366 model units from exact rational points. The distance is chosen for the lattice station below, not for looks: it is 1.8% of the curve size, and |d·κ| ≤ 0.555 against the closest radius 0.0660, so neither rail folds.

The fit, as a number. A cubic chain fitted to the same exact rail needs 45 spans to reach 9.19e-06, which is 4.44e-06 of the curve size (2.068 model units, the bounding-box diagonal). It is not drawn. The exact rail costs one rational expression; the cubic version costs 45 spans and still misses.

The lattice: where the hits begin

The spine. At its own pitch 105 the curve has exactly 2 lattice points — the two ends (0, 0) and (66, 140) at scale 105. A search of every rational parameter t = k/q up to q = 200 finds no smaller interior hit.

The first interior hit is at t = 1/2, the midpoint, and it needs grid 3360 — 32 times the pitch. At that scale the spine has 3 points:

The mirror station

Where it is. The midpoint is C(1/2) = (-1849/3360, 59/48) = (-0.5503, 1.2292). At grid 3360 it is the integer point (-1849, 4130). Translating the curve by +1849/3360 puts it exactly on the y axis at (0, 4130). The two exact rails land on the same grid at the same station:

curvepoint at grid 3360
spine(0, 4130)
rail +d(120, 4103)
rail -d(-120, 4157)

So the station is one grid where the spine and both rails are whole-number points, and the spine's point sits on the y axis after one integer translation. That is the precise branch station this curve can give.

But the y axis does not bisect it. Measured: the curve is not symmetric about the midpoint (half-turn gap 37% of the size, vertical-mirror gap 49%); the tangent at the station is -102.7°, which is 12.7° off vertical; the inflection is at t = 0.5051, not 1/2; and the line x = 0 meets the curve at four parameters (0.0698, 0.3263, 0.5000, 0.6986). It is a station on the centreline, not a mirror line.

A true mirror axis needs a symmetric curve. The Pythagorean integer cubic family is half-turn symmetric with its midpoint at (c, 0) — a lattice point at scale 1, at its inflection, with a horizontal tangent there — but it is not PH, so its rail is fitted, not exact. A symmetric PH curve is the third option and is not built.

What this means for branches

On the spine. At grid 3360 there is exactly one interior station, the midpoint. Branches that need several precise points need a larger grid (25,515 gives t = 1/3, 76,545 gives t = 2/3) or a different curve.

On the rails. A rail shares this station only because d was chosen for it. The first hit of the rail at the unchosen distance 3/100 = 0.030 needs grid 688,800. The rule is arithmetic: the rail's normal at the station is (40/41, −9/41), so d must carry the factor 41 to land on the grid. A rail at an arbitrary distance does not hit the lattice at any usable scale.

Side curves that are not rails. A branch is its own curve, not a parallel offset, so it is not bound by the spine's normal: build it with whole-number data on the grid you need and it can hit the lattice by construction. The rails are the only side curves where the hit is dictated by the parent.
