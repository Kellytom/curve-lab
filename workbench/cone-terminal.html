Fig. C1: the two-arc cone terminal

The end of a ribbon, closed by two arcs instead of a cap. Each arc leaves a rail end, both have radius R = 2 × half width, and they meet at a point on the centreline √3 × half beyond it. Each arc is a 60° turn, so the pair is 120° — exactly the cap limit in docs/PHILOSOPHY.md. The tip is a point, and that is the point of a cone.

Fig. C1a — the default cap, on a leaned pair of rails

The two rail ends disagree by 0.3 model units along the stroke, which is what a curved spine does. The cap is symmetric about its own axis, but that axis sits off the stroke: the tip is at x = 0.000, not 0. This is the "slightly offset" the operator saw.

Fig. C1b — the symmetric cap

With symmetric=True, the stroke's end is the cap's midpoint and the stroke's tangent is its axis. Each rail end is snapped to its mirror position at the ribbon's true half width (40.000), the tip lands on the centreline, and the page reports what moved: the + rail moved 0.000 and the − rail 12.000, axis twist 0.00°.
