Quintic Béziers, every join G3

A join is G3 when position, tangent, curvature and curvature-rate agree. Magnify a join and the two spans stay indistinguishable in where they are, where they head, how hard they bend, and how smoothly that bend is accelerating. The spans here are independent Béziers: each has its own control points, and every join passes the measured gate. The tables are the gate — a row that failed would remove the curve from the page.

Independent spans, not one cut-up cubic. G3 is the requirement; the spans do not have to be pieces of one polynomial. Where the curve itself is a cubic (Figs. G3.3 and G3.5), the spans collapse to cubics. Where it is not (the bell, the log spiral, the sine wave), the spans are quintic, because a generic chain of independent cubics cannot carry G3: a cubic has four control points against the five join conditions, while a quintic has six and leaves two handles free for the fit. The four-cubic bell — four independent cubics — is the rejected case on the G3 notes page, together with the circle, which is G3 everywhere and therefore too easy for the showcase. The G3 catalogue takes the named curves, hearts, presets, primals and recovered spines through the same quintic interpolation and records what is admitted and what is not.

A log spiral has curvature and curvature-rate defined everywhere, so its quintic spans can be G3-joined without a special case. The rate of change of curvature is the 'smooth acceleration' of the bend; it matches through every join, not just the curvature itself.

A second cubic, so the five spans collapse to a cubic again. Every quintic control point is an integer after ×6250. The integer controls are a lattice statement about the handles, not a claim that the curve is integer between the knots.

The gate, and what it refuses

Each table is computed from the Bézier derivatives at the join, not from a sampled polyline: position gap, tangent angle, curvature gap and curvature-rate gap. The pass threshold is 1e-9 for the three scalar gaps and 1e-6° for the tangent angle (floating-point noise). The integer cubic's controls are integers at ×3125 (the ogee) and ×6250 (the lattice cubic); that is a lattice statement about the handles, not a claim that the curve is integer between the joins.
