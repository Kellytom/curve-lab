Cubic Béziers, every join G3

Cubic Béziers only. Every span has four control points; every join is G3 in position, tangent, curvature and curvature-rate. These are the cubic solutions: the curve is a cubic (or one cubic polynomial cut into pieces), so the quintic G3 representation collapses to a cubic. Inkscape and the glyph pipeline need four-point spans, so this is the page they read; the non-cubic curves — the bell, the log spiral, the sine wave — stay on Quintic Béziers every join G3.

Each panel reads left to right: the curve; its G3 acceleration dκ/ds with the cuts marked; then the construction pipeline — ideal, G3 interpolation, integer cubic spans at ×625.

Why cubic only. A generic cubic cannot carry a G3 join: four control points against the five join conditions. The cases here are the ones where it can — the whole curve is one cubic polynomial, cut at the fifths, so every cut is the same polynomial and all derivatives agree. The quintic G3 span is a degree elevation of each piece; the page shows the collapsed four-point form. The four-cubic bell — four independent cubics — is the rejected case on the G3 notes page, together with the circle, which is G3 everywhere and therefore too easy. The G3 catalogue records which other curves admit a quintic interpolation; this page only carries the cubic ones.

The bell is not a cubic, so it cannot collapse to one. Eight independent four-point spans, solved for G3 at every join, carry it with a worst Δ(dκ/ds) of 6e-13 and a peak error of 7.5e-6. The gate, and what it refuses

Each table is computed from the Bézier derivatives at the join, not from a sampled polyline: position gap, tangent angle, curvature gap and curvature-rate gap. The pass threshold is 1e-9 for the three scalar gaps and 1e-6° for the tangent angle (floating-point noise). The integer cubic's controls are integers at ×625 on every panel; that is a lattice statement about the handles, not a claim that the curve is integer between the cuts.
