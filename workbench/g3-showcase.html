Cubic Béziers, every join G3

Cubic Béziers only. Every span has four control points; every join is G3 in position, tangent, curvature and curvature-rate. These are the cubic solutions: the curve is a cubic (or one cubic polynomial cut into pieces), so the quintic G3 representation collapses to a cubic. The bell is the exception: it is not a cubic, so it is carried as an independent eight-span cubic chain, solved for G3; its control points are real, not integer. Inkscape and the glyph pipeline need four-point spans, so this is the page they read; the other non-cubic curves — the log spiral and the sine wave — stay on Quintic Béziers every join G3.

Each panel reads left to right: the curve; its G3 acceleration dκ/ds with the cuts marked; then the construction pipeline — ideal, G3 interpolation, integer cubic spans at ×625.

Why cubic only. A generic cubic cannot carry a G3 join: four control points against the five join conditions. The cases here are the ones where it can — the whole curve is one cubic polynomial, cut at the fifths, so every cut is the same polynomial and all derivatives agree. The quintic G3 span is a degree elevation of each piece; the page shows the collapsed four-point form. The bell is not a cubic, so it cannot collapse: eight independent four-point spans, solved for G3, carry it at a peak error of 7.5e-6. The four-cubic bell is the rejected case; the eight-span chain is the passing one. The circle is G3 everywhere and is explained on the G3 notes page. The G3 catalogue records which other curves admit a quintic interpolation; this page only carries the cubic ones.

The bell is not a cubic, so it cannot collapse to one. Eight independent four-point spans, solved for G3 at every join, carry it with a worst Δ(dκ/ds) of 6e-13 and a peak error of 7.5e-6. The four-cubic attempt is the rejected case; eight spans are what it takes. The control points are real, not integer.

The gate, and what it refuses

Each table is computed from the Bézier derivatives at the join, not from a sampled polyline: position gap, tangent angle, curvature gap and curvature-rate gap. The pass threshold is 1e-9 for the three scalar gaps and 1e-6° for the tangent angle (floating-point noise). The integer cubic's controls are integers at ×625 on the five integer panels; that is a lattice statement about the handles, not a claim that the curve is integer between the cuts. The bell's controls are real; only its span count and G3 data are fixed.

Cusp curves · allowed with a declared cusp

A cusp is a declared feature, not a G3 join: the curve is split into smooth arcs between cusps, and G3 applies to the arcs. The astroid is admitted as four such arcs; the other -oids and the cardioid are already admitted. Cusped hearts: heart · Eisemann lower, heart · Hechinger lower, heart · Kuriscak lower, heart · Kuriscak upper, heart · Mangaldan, heart · Sio lower, heart · Sio upper, heart · Valentine, heart · Wolfram, heart · Wu–Nick lower, heart · cardioid, heart · half-ellipse lower, heart · |x|^(2/3) lower, heart · |x|^(2/3) upper. These are quintic cases, not cubic panels.
