Cubic Béziers, every join G3

Cubic Béziers only. Every span has four control points; every join is G3 in position, tangent, curvature and curvature-rate. These are the cubic solutions: the curve is a cubic (or one cubic polynomial cut into pieces), so the quintic G3 representation collapses to a cubic. The bell is the exception: it is not a cubic, so it is carried as an independent eight-span cubic chain, solved for G3; its control points are real, not integer. Inkscape and the glyph pipeline need four-point spans, so this is the page they read; the other non-cubic curves — the log spiral and the sine wave — stay on Quintic Béziers every join G3.

Each panel reads left to right: the curve; its G3 acceleration dκ/ds with the cuts marked; then the construction pipeline — ideal, G3 interpolation, integer cubic spans at ×625.

Why cubic only. A generic cubic cannot carry a G3 join: four control points against the five join conditions. The cases here are the ones where it can — the whole curve is one cubic polynomial, cut at the fifths, so every cut is the same polynomial and all derivatives agree. The quintic G3 span is a degree elevation of each piece; the page shows the collapsed four-point form. The bell is not a cubic, so it cannot collapse: eight independent four-point spans, solved for G3, carry it at a peak error of 7.5e-6. The four-cubic bell is the rejected case; the eight-span chain is the passing one. The circle is G3 everywhere and is explained on the G3 notes page. The G3 catalogue records which other curves admit a quintic interpolation; this page only carries the cubic ones.

The finished curve · what can be handed over

The unit is the finished curve, not the span. A curve is G3-complete when every interior join — including the closure seam of a closed form and every mirror seam — matches position, tangent, curvature and curvature-rate, and every break is declared by the source (a cusp, a node, or a singular endpoint), not created by the fit. A smooth handover is a G3-complete curve that is one path with no declared feature and whose deviation is under 5% of its size. That is the list to give Inkscape or a glyph editor without a smoothness caveat; a curve with a declared cusp or node is still complete, but the feature is what the editor must know about.

The quintic is the carrier, not the goal. Fixing both endpoints, a G3 join asks for six conditions — tangent direction, curvature and curvature-rate at each end — against eight unknowns in a quintic span; the two left over are the handle lengths used to follow the source. A quartic has no freedom left; a cubic is over-determined. So a quintic span is the least span that can meet G3 at both ends. How many spans there are is a property of the assembly — mirror seams, cusps, nodes and fidelity knots — not of the degree.

Mirror seams are benign, and testable. If a curve crosses a mirror axis orthogonally, reflection preserves curvature (κ is a scalar invariant), so κ is continuous; κ is even about the seam, so dκ/ds is odd and must vanish there. A true mirror of a smooth half is therefore G3 at the seam automatically, and dκ/ds = 0 is the test that the mirror is true. An oblique crossing fails even G1; a mirror tangent — the two arcs meeting with opposite tangents — is a cusp, not a smooth seam, and is declared as one.

The closure seam is not an exemption. Where a closed curve starts is an artifact of the parameterisation, so the seam must pass like any other join. If it fails, re-knot the decomposition or place the seam at a declared node or cusp. The nodal loops (bow, strophoid, bernoulli, nodal, abs-Gerono) declare a node at the seam: complete with that feature, not smooth.

Native degree. A quintic span is a pure degree elevation of a cubic when its fifth divided difference vanishes: P0 − 5P1 + 10P2 − 10P3 + 5P4 − P5 = 0. The fifth-degree freedom then did no work, and the row is badged cubic-native — a simple curve collapsing to a cubic is a selector signal, not an accident. The badge is derived from the control polygon, never asserted. 21 catalogue rows are cubic-native; all five cubic panels on this page are, by construction.

The gate, and what it refuses

Each table is computed from the Bézier derivatives at the join, not from a sampled polyline: position gap, tangent angle, curvature gap and curvature-rate gap. The pass threshold is 1e-9 for the three scalar gaps and 1e-6° for the tangent angle (floating-point noise). The integer cubic's controls are integers at ×625 on the five integer panels; that is a lattice statement about the handles, not a claim that the curve is integer between the cuts. The bell's controls are real; only its span count and G3 data are fixed.
