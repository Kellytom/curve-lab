Evolute parallel involutes

One evolute; every offset is an involute of it. Pick a family — LAC, cycloid, roulette, ellipse, astroid, circle involute, catenary, parabola, semicubical, or a PH cubic — then change d.

base C evolute E (main frame local, inset full) +d parallel −d parallel

The construction

1. The base. A family curve C(t); the tangent and signed curvature are read off the samples. For the LAC, curvature is κ(s) = s^−α and the heading is θ(s) = s^(1−α)/(1−α) (α ≠ 1) or ln s at α = 1, then the curve is integrated from that heading.

2. The evolute. E = C + ρN with ρ = 1/κ and N the unit normal. Every normal of C is a radius of the evolute. For the LAC the evolute is itself a LAC with α_E = 2 − 1/α: α = −1 (Euler/clothoid) → 3, α = 1 (log spiral) → 1. For the other families the same centre-of-curvature construction gives the classical evolutes: cycloid → congruent cycloid, ellipse → astroid, astroid → astroid, circle involute → circle, catenary → tractrix, parabola → semicubical.

3. The offsets. The exact two-sided offsets are

Pd(t) = C(t) + d·N(t)

Every one is also an involute of the evolute. Between cusps of E, with T_E = ±N and s_E the arc length of E,

Pd(t) = E(t) − (sE(t) + d₀ + d) TE(t)

and the string length s_E + d₀ is the radius of curvature ρ. The canvas draws the exact normal form, so it stays correct through the evolute cusps of the ellipse, astroid, cycloid and roulettes; the involute-string form is read piecewise between those cusps.

Semicubical parabola and the symmetric-X Bézier

Yes — the semicubical parabola is the cusp normal form, and the symmetric-X cubic Bézier is a cuspidal cubic, i.e. the semicubical up to an affine change of coordinates. The Neile parabola is y² = x³, equivalently (t², t³); it is the evolute of the parabola. All ordinary cusps are locally y² = x³, and every cuspidal cubic is projectively — and, with the cusp and its tangent placed, affinely — the semicubical.

The symmetric X P₀ = (−1,−1), P₁ = (1,1), P₂ = (−1,1), P₃ = (1,−1) has B′(½) = 0: the velocity vanishes at the centre, so it is a cusp, not a self-crossing. Its local expansion is y ∼ t², x ∼ t³ — the semicubical cusp. The canonical exact control points for x = t², y = t³ on t ∈ [0,1] are

P₀ = P₁ = (0,0) · P₂ = (1/3, 0) · P₃ = (1, 1)

so the replication is exact, not an approximation. It is not the Tschirnhausen cubic: that one is a nodal cubic (a loop, the node angle 60°), the PH cubic. A symmetric-X control polygon whose arms cross with a non-zero velocity is a node; the zero-velocity case is the semicubical cusp. The page's parabola → semicubical family draws the cusp from the parabola's evolute.

Notes

The α, k and b parameters are read from the family selector; the URL accepts ?family=, ?alpha= and ?d=. The α selector switches between Euler/clothoid, a log spiral and intermediate LACs. α = 0 is excluded: its evolute is a point, so the involute construction has no tangent.

The offset distance is a signed normal distance; the canvas fits the base and the two ±d curves, so the evolute can run off the frame when it is much larger than the base. A large d will fold where the offset reaches a centre of curvature; keep d below the smallest radius of curvature of the base. The roulettes, astroid and cycloid have cusps, so their curvature (and evolute radius) blows up at the cusp; the samples avoid the exact cusp point.

PH curves. A Pythagorean-hodograph curve has P′ = (u² − v², 2uv), so its unit normal is rational and its offset is exact: P_d = P + d·(−2uv, u² − v²)/(u² + v²). The selector includes a sample PH cubic, u = 1, v = t, whose two offsets are drawn by that exact normal. The four integer presets (S01–S04) and their controls live on the generators page; the full exact offset rail for the stem library belongs on stem-library-offset.html.
