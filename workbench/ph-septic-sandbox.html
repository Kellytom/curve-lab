Demand 6 and 7: the two curves

A polynomial PH curve has odd degree. With r' = w^2, a speed of degree n gives a position of degree 2n + 1: 5 for a quadratic speed, 7 for a cubic speed. Degree 6 is therefore not a polynomial PH curve at all - which is why this page shows two different things, side by side.

A is the general degree-7 polynomial: both axes free, seven n_k each, no PH promise. B is the PH septic: a cubic speed, so the curve keeps rational offsets and exact integer hits.

A | the general polynomial

x(t) = span * sum n_k t^k and the same for y, k = 1..7. Positive low orders lift, negative high orders brake, and the mix is what makes an arch or an S rather than a ramp. The control vectors u_k = n_k * span * k! are the derivatives at t = 0.

B | the PH septic

w = (b_x0 + b_x1 t + b_x2 t^2 + b_x3 t^3) + i (b_y0 + ... + b_y3 t^3), and r = integral of w^2. Degree 7, rational offsets, exact pitch and integer hits - the same machinery as the quintic, one degree up.

The diagram is the same one Q13 draws for the quintic, one degree up: the seven vectors T_m = (w^2)_m / (m+1) head to tail, the dashed line their sum. Shape the polygon into a triangle or a rectangle and the curve follows.

What the difference costs

Panel A can make any degree-7 shape, including the even/odd split that a sextic or septic control pair wants: the t^6 term lifts the middle without skewing the ends, the t^7 term tilts one end against the other. What it cannot do is promise a rational offset or an integer lattice.

Panel B keeps those promises but has fewer knobs: a cubic speed is eight real numbers, and the position degree-7 terms are not free - they are the square of the speed. The trade is exactness for control.
