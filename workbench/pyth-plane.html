Hyperbolae, factorisation, and a parabola's parallels

The level sets in the p-triple plane are not decoration: a fixed a or a fixed b is a hyperbola whose integer points are exactly the factorisations of that number. And the parabola in that plane has an exact parallel family — reached through its evolute, not through an offset formula.

Fig. P1 · the (m, n) plane: a and b are hyperbolae

Both families are hyperbolae. A fixed a has vertex at (√a, 0) and asymptote n = m; a fixed b is n = b/2m. Near the origin the curves are sharp; far out they flatten.

The radius of curvature at the vertex is exactly √a, so the "sharp near 0,0" impression is a fact: it grows as the square root of the level.

Fig. P2 · the (a, b) plane: fixed m is a parabola, not a hyperbola

Eliminating n at fixed m gives a = m² − b²/(4m²). The sideways curvature in the plot is this parabola, not a hyperbola; the hyperbolae live in the (m,n) plane above.

Fig. P3 · the parabola's evolute and its exact parallels

Take the evolute, then take its involute with a shift in arc length and you get the parabola back (shift −1, error 2.0e-15) or, for any other shift, an exact parallel of it.

But a parallel of a parabola is not a parabola. Fitting a parabola to the offset leaves a residual of 2.1% of d at d = 0.1 and 11.3% of d at d = 0.5 (the offset folds beyond d = 1, the vertex radius):

So the involute/evolute route gives the parallels exactly — and proves, rather than denies, that they are a different family. There is no parallel parabola.

Fig. P4 · the evolute at integer t (y = x², not x²/2)

Two parabolas, two evolutes, and one correction. For y = x² the evolute is (−4t³, 3t² + ½), i.e. 27x² = 16(y − ½)³. For the page's y = x²/2 it is x² = (y − ½)³ (Fig. P3). The familiar 27x² = 4(y − 2)³ with (2t³, 3t² + 2) is a semicubical parabola, but it is not this evolute — it is the same curve with x doubled and y shifted by 1.5.
