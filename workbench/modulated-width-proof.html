Modulated width proof

The width is a declared function, not a parallel offset. The measurement below is the difference between the two ideas.

The law. w(u) = w0 (1 + 64a u³(1−u)³), with a = 0.6 and w0 = 1. The law, its slope and its curvature all meet the plain stroke at both ends, so the band is continuous at the seams. The two rails are built from it; they are not equidistant.

The comparison. The dashed lines are a constant band at w0, the 2 × parallel assumption. The solid rails leave it by up to 0.300 of a half-width. That difference is the proof: this curve cannot be put in the 2 × parallel rule without making the rule false.

What this proves

A variable width is not a wide stroke and not a parallel band. It is a width function whose rails happen to be smooth here, with no fold and no self-crossing. The measured seam is exact because the law was chosen to meet the plain stroke there — that is a design requirement, not a coincidence.
