curve-lab · G3 terminals

Extend, then cut at the station

A stem or an ending often stops at a station on a longer smooth curve. The conveyor fits the longer run, finds the span containing the station, and splits it with de Casteljau. The kept chain's new end is interior to a G3 chain, so it inherits the G3 data; the discarded tail is drawn only to prove the curve continued. This is the third case in the G3 rule — the first two are a smooth natural end (land on it) and a singular end (trim or reject).

The cut is a station, not a singularity. The page measures the cut point's distance from the ideal, the tangent, curvature and curvature-rate errors, and the G3 defect across the split before the tail is thrown away. The cut gate is 1% of the curve size, 1° of tangent, 0.05 of curvature, 0.1 of curvature-rate, and 1e-9 across the split; otherwise the curve should be trimmed or rejected, not cut to hide an overshoot.
