curve-lab · a stem with a width that is exact

Tapered stem from the log-spiral band

Two spirals with the same pitch bound a band whose width is proportional to the radius. The rails are exact, the taper is exact, and the width is not symmetric about the centreline — that asymmetry is the price, and it is measured below.

Fig. T1 · the band, its centreline, and a cone terminal

The rails are not offsets of the centreline; they are the two spirals themselves. That is what makes them exact.

What is exact, and what is not

Exact: both rails are log spirals of the same pitch; the ratio width / r is constant at 0.0532 on the inside and 0.0560 on the outside — measured to 1e-9 at every station.

Not exact: the band is asymmetric. The outer half-width exceeds the inner by 5.25% of their mean. A symmetric stem would need an offset of the centreline, which is the thing this construction exists to avoid.

The cone terminal is placed at the mean half-width; each rail therefore misses the cone base by 2.63% of the offset. On a k = 1.12 band that is a fraction of a millimetre at this scale, but it is not zero, and it is the seam a real design has to declare.

Where it belongs

This is the only exact variable-width stem in the catalogue. It is the right base for a tapered stalk, a seagrass blade or a volute; it is the wrong base for anything that must land on the integer lattice, because every coordinate is transcendental. Pair it with a rational connector at a declared seam, rather than pretending it is on a grid.
