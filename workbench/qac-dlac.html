qAC: one constant past the LAC — and its discrete analogue

The log-aesthetic curve is defined by qα = c₀s + c₁ in arc length. Its similarity curvature u satisfies u′ = (α−1)u²; adding a constant gives the quasi-aesthetic curve, u′ = (α−1)u² + c, and c = 0 is exactly the LAC. The discrete LAC is the same idea on equal turns: constant turning angle, segment ratios from (uₙ₊₁)a + 1/(uₙ)a = C, with a = α−1. It is a curve in its own right, not a sampled approximation.

Fig. Q1 · three qAC curves

c = 0 recovers the LAC. The check on this page integrates the LAC form and measures qα against arc length: it is linear to 4.8e-05 over α = 1.5, 2, 3, which is the definition holding numerically.

Fig. Q2 · two discrete LACs

The discrete curve is the paper's recurrence: the turning is constant, the segment lengths are the ratios, and the shape is generated without integrating anything. This is the form that belongs on a lattice, because every step is a turn and a length, not a Fresnel integral.

Where it sits

These are the paper's two extensions (arXiv 1808.03104). The repo's own LAC dial is the curvature exponent p, with κ ∝ sp; the paper's slope is α, with qα = c₀s + c₁, so p = −1/α. The Gen-Arch page uses the same mapping for its radial: c = α − 1.

Neither family is exact on the lattice: both are integrated or iterated numerically, so they are declared, not named. The discrete one is the promising road — a constant-turn, ratio-driven curve can be built with rational lengths and Pythagorean turns, which is exactly what the stem cuts already do.
