The named curves and heart forms, as G3 quintic interpolations

This page shows the interpolation, not the ideal curve. Each admitted curve is a chain of quintic Bézier spans, six or ten spans per branch, with every join measured G3: position, direction, bend, and the smooth acceleration of the bend. The ideal curve is the faint dashed line underneath. The named curves are read from their exact homogeneous control polygons; the heart forms are read from a local cubic fit of their sampled polylines. A branch that cannot be interpolated G3 — a cusp, a point at infinity, a singular handle solve — is rejected and named in the table, not quietly dropped.

How admission works. The knots are placed at equal arc length on the curve. At each knot the tangent, curvature and curvature-rate are read from the exact homogeneous control polygon, or from a local cubic fit for the sampled hearts. The quintic span matches them at both ends, and the two handle lengths are chosen to minimise the deviation from the ideal. The table's max deviation is a geometric distance, not a parameter error. A curve is admitted only when the deviation is under 5% of its own size; the rejected reasons state the measured share. The G3 gate is computed from the Bézier derivatives, not from the drawn polyline.

Admitted curves

Rejected, with the reason

A cusp has no tangent direction, so G3 is undefined there; a point at infinity has no finite position. These are not failures of the interpolation — they are curves outside the G3 domain. A few hearts are rejected because the quintic G3 spans cannot follow their sharp features within 5% of the curve size; the reason gives the measured share. The squircle is still sampled only and needs the same polyline treatment. The next pass adds it and tightens the high-error cases with more spans.
