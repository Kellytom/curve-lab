The named curves, as G3 quintic interpolations

This page shows the interpolation, not the ideal curve. Each admitted curve is a chain of quintic Bézier spans, 6 spans per branch, with every join measured G3: position, direction, bend, and the smooth acceleration of the bend. The ideal curve is the faint dashed line underneath. A branch that cannot be interpolated G3 — a cusp, a point at infinity, a singular handle solve — is rejected and named in the table, not quietly dropped.

How admission works. The knots are placed at equal arc length on the exact curve. At each knot the tangent, curvature and curvature-rate are read from the exact homogeneous control polygon; the quintic span matches them at both ends, and the two handle lengths are chosen to minimise the deviation from the ideal. The table's max deviation is a geometric distance, not a parameter error. The G3 gate is computed from the Bézier derivatives, not from the drawn polyline.

Admitted curves

Rejected, with the reason

A cusp has no tangent direction, so G3 is undefined there; a point at infinity has no finite position. These are not failures of the interpolation — they are curves outside the G3 domain. The sampled curves (heart, squircle) are not yet fitted; they need a smooth polyline spline first. The next pass adds those, and more span counts.
