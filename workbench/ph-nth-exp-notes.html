Knot experiments that did not work

Three controls were built on the experimental sandbox to shorten the legs of a curve. They were taken off it because they cannot work on this family, and the reason is a measurement, not an opinion. The buttons below are shown, disabled, so the shape of the attempt is not lost.

Why they cannot work

The legs are not a separable stretch of the parameter. A curve's self-crossings — the knot — run from about t = 0.02 to t = 0.98. Any window that removes a leg from `t` therefore also removes crossings, which changes the knot rather than tidying it.

The rebuild has a second limit: it is exact only over a span whose length is the square of a fraction, and the largest such span below the whole curve is (15/16)² = 0.879 — shorter than the 0.96 the knot occupies. So the only exact span that holds the knot is the whole curve, and the rebuild says so instead of cutting.

A third limit is the exponent step: at step 1/2 a row is a polynomial in √t, so substituting t = a + span·u does not give a polynomial at all. The rebuild is undefined there and now asks for step 1.

The three controls, as they were

controlwhat it didmeasured result
show only the knotset the `t` window to the outermost self-crossings cut t 1/48 … 47/48 — two per cent off each end, i.e. nothing, because the crossings span the curve
rebuild without the legsrebuild the crossing span as a new exact curve “the span needed is the whole curve, so nothing was cut”
cut and rebuildcut a fraction of arc length off each end, then rebuild the same: the needed span has no exact square length below the whole curve

The exact rebuild itself is sound and was checked against a Python reference to the integer: the browser produced [8017920, −50761728, 40310784] for the same span. It is the ilegibility of these curves for a short span, not the arithmetic, that parked them.

What replaces them

Shorter legs come from the numbers, not from a cut. Measured on one curve, moving the two outer flat spots inward takes the legs from 88% of the figure to 42% and then 12%, and the knot from 12% to 57% to 87%.

At the Bezier step the question dissolves. A leg is nearly straight, so one cubic span covers it to any sane tolerance, while the knot needs several. Spending spans where the curvature is gives short legs and a faithful knot without cutting the curve at all — which is the plan recorded in TODO.md.

Tweaking the primary curve: what the buttons do

Pick a change in the dropdown, then press one of the four buttons; each press adds that change once more, so ten presses give ten times the nudge. Every pattern is balanced about the middle, so the curve stays mirrored: x: t1, t2 and x: t2 to t4 push the width, x: t3 to t6 and the two y patterns push the vertical gesture. nudge is one step, bump is five. A minus button subtracts the pattern, which is just as safe as adding it — the rows already carry negative numbers. The status line prints the grid after every press, because the grid after a change depends on arithmetic and not on how round the number looks: on 1/15 a nudge of 3 keeps 1/154, of 5 gives 1/1386 and of 6 gives 1/770. Expand and change multiplies both rows by 1 + s(t - t^2), which re-times the stroke rather than adding a term. It used to keep the two new powers, and those brought the divisors 13, 14 and 15 into the grid — 1/210 at best, against a base of 1/154. It now fits the widened rows back into the numbers that already exist and rounds them to whole numbers, so the degree does not change. The price is distortion, and the status line prints it — the worst miss as a share of the row, and the grid the new whole numbers give, which is not promised to stay put. Press it twice and the second press widens what the first produced.

This text lived under the tweak panel on the sandbox until 2026-11-16, where it was longer than the control it described. The panel now carries the three numbers that matter — the pattern, the direction and the step — and this page carries the reasoning.

Failed: the census families on the sandbox

The census (workbench/family-census.json) measured 7,068 symmetric flat-spot curves and found better knots than the sections above carry: nine crossings at grid 1/42, six at grid 1/36. Twelve of them were wired into a section on the sandbox, "Best of the census: every copy count". The section is withdrawn.

The reason is arithmetic, not the census. A curve with three outer pairs and five copies at 1/2 has eleven flat spots, so its row runs to t^11 and the coefficients reach 1013 to 1023. The page draws in double precision: the knot is 10-13 of the row's size, so the whole picture collapses to a single point. Measured on the page: frame span 2×10-6, all 701 path points at the same coordinate.

Two things are true at once: the census numbers are exact (whole-number arithmetic, no sampling), and the page cannot show those particular curves. The six cheaper knots in the census — 1/8 + 1/11 at 9 crossings, grid 1/42 — draw correctly and are the ones worth promoting; the eleven-spot families need either a scaled row or an exact renderer before they belong on a page.

The point count is not part of the formula

The view panel shows a line like visible part: 12,001 points over t 0.021 to 0.979 (4×). That number is about the drawing, not about the curve. It says how many sample points the polyline was built from at the current magnification; the curve itself is the row of numbers above the figure, and nothing in the row is changed by pressing recalculate at this zoom.

Why it exists: a picture is a polyline of straight segments, and at 10× or 12× the corners of a 700-point polyline are visible. The button multiplies the detail by four and samples only the window on screen, so a zoomed view looks like a line. Press it again for four times more. Panning and zooming do not resample, so dragging stays cheap.

Why it must not be used later: the cubic spans for the lead are fitted to the numbers, from the speed row and the tolerance the G3 gate asks for. A point count from the screen is a rendering choice, has no tolerance attached, and changes whenever the operator zooms. Treat it as a label on the glass, not a measurement of the curve. The measurements that do travel are bend (arc over chord), grid (the pitch the row needs), the crossing count and the loop window — and those are what a saved record carries.
