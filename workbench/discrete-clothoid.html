curve-lab · the discrete clothoid

A clothoid built from equal chords and p-triple turns

A clothoid has curvature proportional to arc length. With equal chords that is a rule for the turn schedule: Δθ_k ∝ k. Quantise each turn to the nearest p-triple angle and the polygon is exact — rational nodes, right-triangle turns — while the schedule still rises linearly. This is what the spine does when it rounds a turn schedule; here the schedule is a clothoid.

Fig. D1 · the quantised polygon over the ideal clothoid

The polygon is drawn from exact rational nodes; the scale that clears the denominators is 35700305414733608946418538269322401978515625 (44 digits), so the drawing is the rational polygon, not a rounded copy. The dashed curve is the ideal clothoid with the same curvature law.

Fig. D2 · the law survives the quantisation

Each point is a measured turn; the dashed line is Δθ ∝ s. The fitted exponent is 1.001 against the ideal 1, and the largest quantisation error is 0.31° — under half the 1.35° gap of the 497-angle ladder (c ≤ 3125). Rising schedule: the law holds. The Euclid chain is the falling case — its turns decrease and its curvature exponent drifts to −4/3 (showcase, Fig. 6c) — which is why it is a curl, not a clothoid.
