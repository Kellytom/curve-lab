Clothoid intermediate curves stage II

A spine is a chain of chords. Everything drawn from it — an offset, a branch — is a plate, and every plate is a construction on the spine, not a copy of it.

Offset parallel curves

Slide every chord by some distance d along its normal, then re-intersect the neighbours. The node angles do not change — a spine turns by 4.35° at every node, and so does every offset of it. What changes is the chord length: each end is trimmed by d·tan(α/2), so a chord of length c becomes c − 2d·tan(α/2).

That subtraction is the whole story. It is a constant, not a ratio, so an offset is not a scaled copy of the spine: the short chords at the hook lose proportionally far more than the long ones at the arm, which means every offset has its own b. Push far enough and the innermost chord is eaten to nothing — at d = c/(2·tan(α/2)) = 41146 the plate closes at a point. That number is the hook's own radius of curvature, c/α = 41166: the plate closes when the offset reaches the centre it is turning about.

Root and branch

The root is a spine. A branch is another spine, scaled down and started at a node along that node's own tangent — which is the outgoing chord direction, so the join is G1 for free: there is no blend to compute and no corner to round. The child then turns by the same angle at every node, only faster, because its chords are shorter.

Where a branch may go is the branch-points question — clearance from the rest of the study. This is how it grows. Each panel is the 6-stack spine turned upright, branching at every node (21 chords, 91°).

Branches start on the left plate, not on the spine: the offset curve at 1406 is where a second curve can be joined without cutting the first one. Every origin is then snapped to the integer lattice, which moves it by at most 0.64 — small against a 3125 chord, but it is a real move, so it is drawn.

Same spine, same child, three rules — and the difference between a fern and a fishbone is only whether the side flips. Depth is the next dial: each child is a spine too, so it branches as well.

Where the cubics fit

A span is a cubic through two nodes. The repo fits it with the parabola rule — arm = chord / (3·cos²(δ/2)), exact for a circular arc — and groups the chords so no span carries more than about 45°, which is how 9 cubics stand in for 45 chords. Nodes are smoothed onto the bisector, so consecutive spans meet G1 and the chain stops reading as a polygon.

But a cubic does not survive offsetting. The exact offset of a cubic is a degree-10 curve, not a cubic. The usual shortcut — slide the four control points along their normals — is an approximation: it lands 4% of the offset distance away from a plate fitted properly, and that share does not shrink as the offset gets smaller. It is a shape error, not a rounding error.

Fig. 7 · 9 spans on the spine (ink) · the plate at d = 12344, spans fitted to the offset spine (dark) · the same plate by moving the four control points (gold) · worst gap 4% of the offset, on span 1 of 9

So the order is fixed: offset the spine first, then fit spans to the plate that comes out. Offsetting the cubics and hoping is the one move this geometry punishes.

Next: leaves. A leaf is a plate that stops — one span at the end of a branch, sized by the chord that carries it.

Exact: chords from legs

Everything above is built on a chain that turns by a fixed angle, so its chords run at k·α and every chord has a length of 2ⁿ times a hypotenuse. cos(kα) is irrational, so those nodes are irrational too: no amount of care makes them land on a lattice. That is why the joins had to be snapped, and why the snap was drawn.

An exact spine is built the other way round — the chord is an integer vector, and the angle follows from it:

stepwhat it does
1 · directiontake the ideal heading, snap it to the nearest rung of the triple catalogue
2 · chordlay m·(u, v) for that rung, with m the ideal chord length rounded to whole hypotenuses
3 · offsetslide each chord by d along (−v, u)/h in Fraction arithmetic and intersect the neighbours — exact, no snapping

Every node is now an integer point, and every plate node is an exact rational. The worst direction snap on this spine is 3.7°, and it costs nothing in exactness — the rung you choose is the direction you get.

Fig. 8 · the exact spine (ink) and its plate at d = 1/3 (gold) · every node an integer, every plate node a fraction

The catch, and it is the whole problem: a general rung catalogue puts the plate on a lattice with denominator 10^24 — exact, and useless. The plate is only usable if the rungs share a hypotenuse, because then the shift is integral and the intersections land on a lattice of 1/det. That is the argument for a single-hypotenuse family — the 3125 lattice the rest of the work already sits on — rather than a wide catalogue of triples.
