The workbench

Pick the kind of decision you are making. Each one names the page that starts it; the full list is underneath.

I want a curve

You have a shape in mind, or you need points that a machine can cut. If the numbers are the design — a degree, a few integers, a step — start in Generators, where the curve comes out with whole-number control points and you are told what grid it needs. If you are following a known profile (a hanging chain, a spiral), the fitted studies show how close a small number of cubics gets, and where it stops.

→ Generators

I want a terminal

A stroke that simply stops looks like an accident. A terminal is the decision about how it ends: a cap that closes the end, a taper that thins it, or a point. Start with the piece model, which carries a kind, a reach and a fold, and refuses a terminal that would turn the stroke back on itself. The recovered corpus shows what real ends actually measure.

→ Piece model and terminals

I want a strand

One line that swings — the long curve of a leaf edge, a volute, a drape. A strand is a chain, and the question is always how many segments buy how much fidelity. Start from the recovered spirals: 121 real spines, each tagged with the curve family it was drawn from, so you can see what the old pages actually did before deciding what yours should do.

→ Recovered spirals

I want a sheet

A whole ornament, or a page of them: fleur, acanthus, palmette, and the primals underneath. This is where pieces, strands and terminals are assembled and where the work is ordered. Start with the ornament board: 85 plates, 26 work items and 11 open decisions, with a beginner gloss for every word the other pages assume.

→ Ornament board
