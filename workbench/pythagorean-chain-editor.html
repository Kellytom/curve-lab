Triple-tangent chain · integer editor

The chain is built from arm vectors, not points: each triple becomes a tangent Tₖ=(a,−b), the node step is S·(Tₖ+Tₖ₊₁), and the handles are Tₖ. Because both sides of a join use the same vector, C1 is exact whatever you choose here. The two knobs that matter are which triples you take and how long the handles are.

Fig. E2 · triangles stacked along b

Every triangle shares the vertical b leg, so heights step by exactly 2n while widths grow quadratically. The far corners trace the parabola a = b²/(4n²) − n² — that edge is the stalk's taper, not a design choice. The sliders above drive this figure too.

Fig. E3 · triangles chained along b, b on the x axis

b is the x axis. Each triangle is normalised so that its b leg is the x step while its height keeps that triangle's own a/b shape; triangle k starts exactly where triangle k−1 ends, so the apex chain is a rising stem. One more step makes the widths 1, 2, 3 …; equal makes them all 1. n comes from Fig. E1.

What the page is asserting

C1 is exact in every configuration, including the uniform-handle one: the derivative at a join is 3h·Tₖ from both sides. G2 is not free — with a single handle length the curvature jumps; the per-node handles button solves Cₖ(S−hₖ)=Cₖ₊₁(S−hₖ₊₂), which drives the measured gap to zero but makes the handles rational, so the control points stop being integers.

Integer C1, rational G2 — the same trade the page pythagorean-families.html records in Fig. C1/C2. Model: curve_lab/pyth_chain.py. This page recomputes everything live in JavaScript; if the two ever disagree, the page is wrong.
