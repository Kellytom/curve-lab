Volute proof 01

A scroll from the exact spine, finished by the plant grammar: two rails, a cone terminal, and a branch with its own ending.

The chain. 33 equal chords on the 79-3120-3121 triangle, each turned by a whole multiple of 1.4504°. Every node is an exact rational point; the peak heading is 765.8°, a little over two turns. This is the spine, not a fitted spiral.

The grammar. The rails stand at ±0.26, so the band is 2d = 0.52 wide. The open tip ends in a cone terminal of radius 2 × offset = 0.52. A branch leaves node 12 and ends in seagrass, because a branch inherits the ending rule. No bare tip.

The rails are numeric normal offsets: a log-spiral's exact partner is scaled and rotated, so a constant width here is fitted. The spine is exact; the band is the stated approximation.

The three aspects

Linearity: every chord is straight; the chain is a polygon before it is a curve. Curve: the heading turns by a growing whole-number multiple, so the curvature changes from chord to chord. Infinity: the open spiral tends to a limit heading and a limit circle it never reaches. The volute is a complete design under the rule: all three aspects are present, and none of them is decorative.

Grounded: the start tangent is the declared horizontal; the 90° heading is the vertical. The scroll stands on the one and rises through the other.

Why it is a proof

The volute is not a special drawing: it is the same spine layer that carries the S-curves and the 3-4-5 lobe, with the plant rules applied to it. The rails are offsets, the tip is a terminal, the branch leaves an exact node and gets the same ending vocabulary. The 2 × offset rule and the no-bare-tip rule hold on a scroll, which is the ornament the plant grammar has to survive.
