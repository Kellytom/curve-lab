Fig. 6c · asymptotic

The exponent drifts to −4/3

A curve with κ ∝ sᵖ is a generalised Euler spiral: p = 1 is the clothoid, p = 0 a circle, p = −1 the logarithmic spiral. Fit p on the chain and it is not constant — it walks toward −4/3 as the band widens.

The algebra gives it: the turn is about 2n/m, the step grows like m², so the arc length grows like m³ and the curvature falls like s^(−4/3). n only scales the constant.

  • measured −0.835 (m ≤ 6)
  • −1.325 (m ≤ 400)
  • asymptote −1.333

Fig. 7b · negative

A constant offset is not a log spiral

This is the most useful refusal on the page. Offset a log spiral by a constant distance and the result is not a log spiral: the angle between its tangent and its radius drifts, and the drift grows with d/r.

So "the log spiral has easy offsets" is true only in the sense of scaling — an offset whose width grows with the radius. A constant-width stem cannot be had this way.

  • drift 0.21° at d/r = 0.02
  • 2.59° at d/r = 0.20

Fig. 7c · negative

A parabola's parallel is not a parabola

The parallel family does exist exactly — as the involutes of the parabola's evolute, which is a semicubical parabola. Set the involute's arc-length shift to −1 and the original parabola comes back to 1e-15.

But those parallels are a different family. Fitting a parabola to the offset leaves a residual of about a fifth of d. There is no parallel parabola, and the construction proves it rather than denying it.

  • involute error 1.6e-15
  • best-fit residual 20.8% of d

19 · 3-4-5 exact spine · explanation

Equal chords, whole-number turns, closure by cancellation

This is the plan the rest of the spine work is measured against. Ten equal chords; every step is a rotation by a whole number of 3-4-5 angles, so every node stays rational and every turn is an integer count, not an angle typed in degrees.

The turn schedule 1,2,3,4,5,−5,−4,−3,−2,−1 is the curve. It winds to a peak heading of 553.05°, then unwinds along the same integers and leaves exactly east. Closure is not a fitting convenience: a 3-4-5 angle is irrational as a fraction of a turn, so no accumulation can land on a rational right angle by accident. The turns have to cancel, and they do: 1+2+3+4+5−5−4−3−2−1 = 0.

The three figures in the showcase are three views of that one object — the exact spine (19a), the closure count (19b), and the rotation built without trigonometry (19c). There is no spline in the chain: the drawing is the polygon itself.

  • 10 equal chords, 11 nodes
  • peak heading 553.05°
  • sum of turns = 0
  • 350 rungs to 3125, median gap 0.14°

Not on this showcase

Each absence is a result, not an omission.

Figures are drawn to their own extents; axes and units are inside each panel. Red marks the integer construction and every lattice hit; blue marks the exact rail; the grid is the field.
