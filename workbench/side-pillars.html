The numbers

What the figure is

The object is a pillar — a column with a base, a shaft and a capital — drawn as its outside edge only. The line steps out at the base, steps in to the foot of the shaft, follows a quadratic Bézier up the shaft, and steps out again at the capital. There is no second line and no fill: the field inside the border is empty paper.

The shaft is not a straight taper. It swells against the straight line from foot to top — the classical entasis — and the swell sits at the station you choose, a third of the height by default. The foot stays the widest point of the shaft, so the swell is a bulge in the taper, not a barrel.

The swell is small beside the pillar's height: at the default 1/50 of the foot on a 48-diameter pillar it is well under a pixel on screen. The dotted line is the straight taper it is measured from; the number in the readout is the measurement, and the figure is the proportion.

Thinness is the point. A Roman order is 7 to 10 diameters tall; this border pillar is 48 diameters tall by default, and the page prints the number so the proportion is read, not assumed. Every value is a ratio of whole numbers; the readout prints fractions, and the sliders move in hundredths.
