The exact profile is the chain itself: one straight design segment per chord, one cubic per segment. The practical profile fits one clothoid through each structural run (the parts between the study's corners) and subdivides a run only when the measured error exceeds the tolerance. The drawing above has one pin — the gold dot — chosen on the exact chain as a branch point; it is a hard split with the exact heading, and the rest of the curve is re-fitted around it. Measured deviation 0.32 mm.

How big is that in type terms

A capital C in a serif face is not two or three cubics; that is a geometric ring, not a letter. These are the native segment counts from ten installed serif faces. TrueType stores quadratics, and one cubic reproduces a quadratic exactly by degree elevation, so the count is the cubic equivalent.

So one letter C is 18–42 cubics, and @ is 46–82 and & is 32–94. The practical half at 14 clothoids is in the range of one serif C. The exact half, at 221, is past a whole glyph — and the band, at 722, is past an @ several times over.

Why the exact count was even higher

The first measurement at a 1 degree step gave 1,078 cubics with 351 in the half. Some of that was a real bug: at a segment boundary the backend reports the next segment's tangent, so a corner was corrupting the end of the previous cubic and the fitter kept splitting to hide it. Reading the tangent just inside the segment fixed it: one straight segment now takes one cubic, not nine. The exact profile still uses one segment per chord by design; the practical profile exists to avoid paying for that.
