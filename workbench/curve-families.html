Spirals at dress-curve proportions

8 inches wide, 1 to 5 inches high. Each strip uses the arc of that family whose bounding box is 8:n, so it fills the shape.

Shallow strips are small turns; tall strips are large turns. Compare the asymmetry: a log spiral tightens toward one end by a fixed ratio; a clothoid tightens continuously; a hyperbolic has a long asymptotic arm.

The real hip curve: 24 × 5 with a 180° hook

A 24 × 5 shape is 4.8:1. The gentle log spiral (b = 0.18) and the clothoid only reach about 2:1, but the sharpness is a parameter: a log spiral at b ≈ 1.6, a polynomial spiral κ ∝ s⁵·⁵, and a hyperbolic window all reach 4.8:1 with a long arm and a 180° hook. The three panels are those sharp members; the earlier 8-inch strips use the gentle ones.

The doubling stack

Stack k triangles, then k-1 of the same shape at twice the size, down to one. A doubled triple is the same shape — (474,6232,6250) has the same angle as (237,3116,3125) — so the chord grows and the turn never changes. That is what makes it a log spiral: radius and arc grow together. Read from the tight end it is a hook, then a sweep that runs away.

Two triples carry the work, and both are good:

The angle is the lever: a chord of the wide triple turns 3.7 times as far as one of the thin, so the same schedule gives a compact hook or a long reaching one. Which you want is a choice about the garment, not about the maths.

Turn grows with the square of the count and growth doubles with the count, so the count sets both at once. The wide triple lands on the hook at four stacks: 163°, but only 8× growth over it, so b = 0.73 — a compact hook, rounder than the hip spiral. The thin one needs nine for 196°, and because it has grown 256× over that same turn, b = 1.62, which is the panel's 1.6.

So the angle sets the count, and the count sets the rate: to hold a hook near 180° the wide triple can only afford four stacks, the thin one nine. That is why the two triples land on different curves — same law, same hook, one compact and one reaching. Both are worth keeping for that reason.

The fill column is a bounding-box measure, so it swings when the last chord doubles back over the start — eight stacks of the thin triple do exactly that. Read the turn column for the hook and the fill column for the slot, and expect them to disagree by a stack.

No single b describes either chain: the doubling is constant, but the turn it is spread over shrinks, so the thin curve runs from 1.0 at the hook to 9.1 at the last chord, and the wide one from 0.6 to 2.4. The tables' b is the average over the whole run.
